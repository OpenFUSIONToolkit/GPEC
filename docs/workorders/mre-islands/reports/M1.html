Lede. GPEC now evaluates the steady modified Rutherford equation (MRE, the island-width equation for a neoclassical tearing mode, NTM) on every rational surface of a run. The new submodule src/Tearing/ModifiedRutherford/ computes each term with its own switch and coefficient. The terms are the classical drive, the bootstrap drive with its transport threshold, the curvature term and the ion-polarization term. The submodule writes their sum F(w) on a grid of island widths to gpec.h5 and is reachable from a [Tearing.ModifiedRutherford] deck section or from solve(ModifiedRutherfordProblem(ffs)). The surface geometry behind the bootstrap and transport terms agrees with Fortran RDCON (the resistive version of the DCON stability code) to within 0.22 % on all four DIII-D-like surfaces (§2). It ran on all three baselines (§3). With the polarization term off, as physics.md prescribes by default, F(w) > 0 at all widths above 2 × 10−5 ψN on the 2/1 surfaces of SPARC H-mode and ARC, because Δ′ > 0 there. On SPARC L-mode, F < 0 everywhere. The three term checks pass (§4). Two findings need decisions. The island frequency that physics.md §7 prescribes is 34–52 times the ion diamagnetic frequency on the H-mode 2/1 surfaces, so if the polarization term is switched on it outweighs all the other terms combined (card M1-D1). And no input carries the heat diffusivity χ⊥ that sets the threshold width wd, so the runs use GPEC's default of 1 m²/s (card M1-D2). Neither blocks M2: polarization is off by default, and χ⊥ is a deck key.

1. Orientation

The question. As in M0: for the SPARC and ARC baselines, how large must a seed island be before an NTM grows (marginal width wm), and how large does it saturate (wsat)? M1 builds the function whose zeros those are, F(w), and checks it. It does not yet find the zeros (M2).

What was built. F(w) = Δ′(w) + Δnc(w) + ΔGGJ(w) + Δpol(w; ω), per physics.md §1, with w the full island width in normalized poloidal flux ψN:

  • classical Δ′(w) = Δ′mn (w/2)−2α₋ (−4DI)1/2, the finite-pressure power law of Benjamin et al. NF 2026 Eq. 10, where DI is the Mercier index and α₋ = ½ − (−DI)1/2. Δ′mn is the diagonal of the ForceFreeStates (FFS) Δ′ matrix.
  • bootstrap Δnc = k1Dnc w/(w² + (k1/0.5) wd²), with Dnc the bootstrap coefficient (Hegna 1999) and wd the transport threshold width (Eq. 11, 14). wd ∝ (χ⊥/χ∥)1/4, and with χ∥(w) ∝ 1/w it grows as w1/4.
  • curvature ΔGGJ, the Glasser–Greene–Johnson term, stabilizing when the resistive-interchange index DR < 0 (Eq. 12).
  • polarization Δpol, Benjamin Eq. C.1, ∝ −ω̂(ω̂ − ω̂π), where ω̂ = ω/ω*i is the island frequency over the ion diamagnetic frequency and ω̂π = 1 + ηi. Off by default; a reduced 1/w³ form and a pol_sign switch (physics.md decision D4) are available.

Where the inputs come from. Δ′ and DI, DR, H come from the FFS result. The surface averages in Dnc and wd (trapped fraction ft, R̄², ⟨Bθ⟩, the Schlutt–Hegna Wc prefactor) are a port of the Fortran RDCON mercier.f averages, computed on the same θ grid as GPEC's existing ResistEval geometry. Densities, temperatures, the E×B frequency ωE, Zeff and χ⊥ come from the equilibrium's kinetic profiles, through the same splines the SLAYER inner layer uses. The deck needs a single toroidal mode number n, a run that produces Δ′ (Riccati with vacuum, or Galerkin), and a kinetic file. Otherwise it stops at parse time with a message.

Numerical scheme. Each term is a closed-form function of w evaluated on a log-spaced grid (default 200 points, 10−5 to 0.2 ψN). The output has Tearing/ModifiedRutherford/w, Terms/{F, classical, bootstrap, curvature, polarization, w_d} (surface × width) and PerSurface/ holding 23 surface inputs. Every term is stored whether or not it is switched on, and F sums only the switched-on terms. A [Tearing] section holding only this sub-table runs no layer growth-rate solve.

2. Surface geometry against Fortran RDCON

What was done. Before building the terms, I ported the mercier.f surface averages and compared them with Fortran RDCON run with MRE_flag on the DIII-D-like example. Fortran ran in M0 (job 139670, results/m0_v1_fortran_rdcon/rdcon_output_n1.nc) and Julia here (job 139894). Fortran prints no Zeff. I backed out Zeff = 1.52 from its viscosity ratio and used the same value in Julia.

What it shows. Four of the six quantities agree to 0.05 %. Dnc and Wc, which contain the radial derivatives p′ and q′, agree to 0.13 % and 0.22 %, the largest at q = 5 (ψN = 0.968), where the two codes end the domain differently. The unit test pins the q = 2 values at 0.2 %. No factor of 2π, normalization or sign difference appears. What it does not settle. These are the bootstrap and transport inputs, not Δ′ or the polarization inputs. Fortran RDCON has no polarization term to compare against.

3. F(w) on the three baselines

What was done. One n = 1 run per baseline: Riccati with vacuum, all terms on, polarization included so that it is recorded (job 139897, results/m1_smoke/, decks by reports/scripts/m1_make_smoke.py). The SPARC kinetic files were converted from the OMFIT p-files by the new reports/scripts/pfile_to_kinetic_h5.py (keV → eV, 1020 m−3 → m−3, krad/s → rad/s), with ωE taken from the p-file's omgeb block. Input choices that no file settles:

  • Ions are 50:50 D–T (mean mass 2.5 mp) on all three. The p-files list deuterium and the ARC table lists no species.
  • Zeff comes from GPEC's quasineutral closure with a carbon impurity: 1.00 (SPARC H-mode, where ni = ne), 1.51 (SPARC L-mode) and 1.76 (ARC) at 2/1. The p-files' impurity block (nz1) is zero, so the converter writes no Zeff profile.
  • χ⊥ = 1 m²/s everywhere, GPEC's chi_e_default; see card M1-D2.
  • The sign of omgeb relative to GPEC's ωE is unverified. It enters only through ω̂, which is the subject of card M1-D1.

They are not seed thresholds.

4. The term checks

What it shows. Each term behaves as physics.md states. The classical term relaxes to Rutherford's constant Δ′ in the zero-pressure limit. The bootstrap term has its threshold shape and limits. The polarization term changes sign as Benjamin Eq. C.1 is written: as coded with pol_sign = +1, it is destabilizing for 0 < ω̂ < ω̂π (physics.md D4, option a). Benjamin's own text calls ion-direction rotation in that interval stabilizing; pol_sign = −1 gives that reading, as D4 records. The wording caveat of check (a): PLAN.md asks for "its zero at Δ′(w) = 0". With the toroidal power law, Δ′(w) keeps the sign of Δ′mn at every w. The classical-only F therefore has no zero unless Δ′mn = 0, and the check reduces to the zero-pressure limit, which passes.

5. Island frequency in the polarization term

The result. physics.md §1d defines ω as the island frequency in the frame where Er = 0. §7 sets it to ωE at the surface for the 0.5×/1×/2× rotation cases, with ω = 0 (the stalled island) as a bounding case. The code takes ω = n ωE × omega_factor and divides by ω*i = −n Ti (ni−1dni/dψ)/(Ziψ0), the density-gradient part only. The temperature gradient enters through ω̂π = 1 + ηi. On the H-mode 2/1 surfaces this gives ω̂ = 33.8 (SPARC) and 52.4 (ARC), so ω̂(ω̂ − ω̂π) ≈ 900 and 2500. With the term on, Δpol reaches −10² (SPARC) to −10³ (ARC) near w = 0.01 (figure 2, dotted). That outweighs all the other terms combined and would put an apparent seed threshold at 0.035–0.05 ψN, set by the polarization term alone.

Why that is suspect. Benjamin (App. C, p. 19) scans ω from −ω*i to +ω*i. The polarization theory behind Eq. C.1 (Wilson 1996) assumes an island frequency of order the diamagnetic frequencies. In it, a collisionless island propagates near ω*e(1 + ηe/4), which is ω̂ of order −1 to −2. Benjamin also states (p. 5) that once the island's rotation relative to the Er = 0 frame vanishes, "the stabilising effect is lost". The bulk E×B rotation, which the p-file ωE measures, is not the island's frequency relative to that frame. Reading §7 as "ω = ωE" therefore treats the plasma's E×B rotation as the island's slip, which is a frame confusion. A related point: SLAYER's ω*i includes the temperature gradient, so "ω̂ = 1" in SLAYER means ω̂π here.

Decision M1-D1: what island frequency the polarization term uses

Found. physics.md §7's ω = ωE gives ω̂ = 34–52 on the H-mode 2/1 surfaces, far outside the ±ω*i range Benjamin scans, and the term then outweighs all the others (§5). The term is off by default, so nothing produced so far depends on this choice.

Why it is your call. It changes physics.md §7, and it sets the polarization axis of KR2 (key result 2).

Confidence that ω = ωE mixes frames: high, given Benjamin's own scan range and wording. Between (a) and (c): (a) keeps the M2 check (c) meaningful; (c) is the conservative paper choice.

6. The heat diffusivity behind wd

The result. wd = cwd[(χ⊥/χ∥) Wc/m²]1/4. The three kinetic inputs carry no χe profile, so χ⊥ = 1 m²/s on every surface (chi_e_default, which the SLAYER layer uses too). physics.md D3 sets the uncertainty range as ×0.3–×3 "about the profile value", but no profile value exists. Benjamin (p. 4, after Eq. 14) estimates χ⊥ ∼ κa²/(6τe), with κ the elongation and a the minor radius. τe there is most likely the energy confinement time; the paper does not define it beside the formula. With wd ∝ χ⊥1/4, a factor of 7 in χ⊥ moves wd by 1.6, and wd is already comparable to the widths of interest (table 1).

Decision M1-D2: the nominal χ⊥

Found. No χe profile in any input. The runs use 1 m²/s. physics.md D3's range has no centre value to apply to.

Why it is your call. It fixes the centre of a KR2 uncertainty axis. The same χ⊥ feeds the SLAYER gate (chi_perp_Dc, physics.md §4).

Confidence in (a): medium. The τe in Benjamin's formula is undefined next to it, and I have not verified the design τE values.

Coherence check. Two findings bear on later claims. First, F > 0 at 2/1 on SPARC H-mode and ARC at almost every width, with polarization off. That makes the M2 linear gate decisive for KR1: those surfaces are either linearly unstable (flagged) or stabilized only by terms below the validity floor. KR1's phrasing, "marginal widths compared with seed widths", may need to admit "no metastable window" as an outcome. Second, SPARC L-mode gives F < 0 at every width. That is a clean, stable reference case for the paper.
