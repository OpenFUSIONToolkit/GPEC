M4 is the first key result (KR1): the ratio of the orbit-averaged alpha torque to the surface torque as the orbit width shrinks, in three reactor-class equilibria. Each equilibrium is rescaled into a family in which only the orbit width changes. The ratio goes to 1 as it should, and at the widths of the real machines the two methods differ by 3–30% depending on device and equilibrium.

In brief. At small width the ratio reaches 1 within 1.5% in ARC and SPARC (Figure {{N_SCAN}}). At the physical field it is 0.69 (ARC) and 1.70 (SPARC) on the published q profiles. Both values come from the inner edge of the response, where orbits read perturbation data that was extrapolated below the cut at q = 1.02. On ARC with q₀ raised to 1.1, which needs no cut, the ratio is 0.91. Away from the edges the orbit average keeps each resonance's contribution but moves it inward by about two orbit widths (Section 3); that is the mechanism behind KR1. On the way I found and fixed a defect in forb's energy refinement that put spikes on two surfaces (Section 5). Three cards: the equilibria for the Letter's figure (Q19), accepting that fix, the module change PLAN asks me to card (Q20, stops M4), and the ITER and SPARC sources (Q21).

1. What M4 ran, and how it was checked

The scan. KR1 asks how the orbit torque forb, computed along each alpha's guiding-center orbit, compares with the surface torque fgar, which treats each flux surface on its own (the zero-orbit-width limit). The comparison is made as the orbit width Δb shrinks at fixed everything else. Grad–Shafranov is homogeneous under multiplying the poloidal flux ψ, the toroidal field function F, the plasma current Ip and the field B by λ, with the pressure ×λ²: the shape, the safety factor q(ψ) and β are unchanged, and Δb ∝ 1/λ. So each published gEQDSK becomes a family λ = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 without a new solve (Figure {{N_FAMILY}}). The kinetic profiles are held fixed, so the alpha distribution and its density are the same for every member. The coil currents scale with λ, so δB/B is fixed. This is a numerical orbit-width scan, not a device family. The width quoted below is the radial excursion over the minor radius a of a 3.5 MeV trapped alpha launched at r/a = 0.8 on the outboard midplane: at λ = 1 it is 0.090 (ARC), 0.093 (ITER) and 0.136 (SPARC).

Forcing. As you asked, ARC is driven by its error-field-correction coils from the MHD physics basis (Leuthold et al. 2026, §5.2): two rows of six picture-frame coils outside the TF coils at θ ≈ ±60° from the outboard midplane, 2 m tall, with 6° toroidal gaps, n = 1 phasing, 10 kA × λ. ITER uses its EFCC set (iter_efcm, 50 kA × λ) and SPARC a midplane window-pane row (R = 2.82 m, 1.5 m tall, 20 kA × λ). Figure {{N_COILS}} shows where they sit.

Numerics. 128 pitch × 16 energy nodes (M3 §5); one point at 192 × 24 moves the torque by 0.3% and the energy term by 2.4%. The surface torque has steps and narrow peaks where a bounce resonance enters the energy range. On the 39 Gauss nodes of M3, fgar and forb sampled them on different sides and their totals scattered by ±8% at small width. The runs here use uniform nodes 0.003 apart in ψ over the outer half (0.006 in ITER and the q₀ = 1.1 families), with Gauss panels inside. Each surface is one job on one CPU.

2. The KR1 scan

The figure. Figure {{N_SCAN}} is KR1's figure. The table gives the numbers; a red fraction marks a dense run that was still incomplete when this report was built (surfaces done / total).

{{KR1_TABLE}}

Which collision frequency. Scaling the field leaves the pitch-angle scattering rate ν unchanged. M3's zero-orbit-width test divided ν by the scale factor ("variant B") so that the surface torque stays exactly invariant. For the field scan that turned out to be the wrong choice: with ν/λ the per-surface difference stays at 0.4–0.5 through λ = 256 (Figure {{N_NU}}b), while with ν fixed it falls to 10⁻² (Figure {{N_NU}}a). My reading, not proved: for ℓ ≠ 0 the orbit shift of a bounce resonance and the width ν that smooths it both scale as 1/λ, so with ν/λ their ratio never changes and there is no zero-width limit. Fixed ν is also the physical choice: a stronger field does not slow collisions. PLAN v0.11 records the switch; the ν/λ points stay on the figure as open symbols. The integrated ratios differ little between the two (table rows agree within 0.04), because the per-surface differences change sign along ψ.

The energy scan. Lowering the birth energy at λ = 1 shrinks the width too, but it also changes the distribution, so PLAN asks that it reproduce the field-scan trend at matched width. On ARC with q₀ = 1.1 it does: 0.975 at 0.875 MeV against 0.966 at λ = 2, both Δb/a = 0.044, and 1.015 at 0.219 MeV against 0.983 at λ = 4 (that run partial). On the published q only the 39-node runs exist: 0.836 against 0.914. The 39-node totals scatter by ±8%, so that gap is within their error, and I have not rerun them dense.

3. Where the two torques differ

A ratio near 1 does not mean the torque profiles match. Each orbit is labelled by its launch flux ψ₀ at the field minimum, but it samples the perturbation over its whole width and its resonance sits at its mean flux ψ̄. Figure {{N_SHIFT}} follows one resonance step of fgar, at ψ ≈ 0.825 in ARC, through the family. In forb the same contribution appears inside it, by about 0.03, 0.017 and 0.009 in ψ at λ = 2, 4 and 8: twice the trapped orbit's ψ excursion at r/a = 0.8 (0.017, 0.0085, 0.0042). The peak is taller and narrower but carries the same integral to within the totals' agreement. One surface at λ = 4 taken apart by harmonic shows it is the ℓ = −2 bounce resonance alone (7.8×10⁻⁴ of 7.8×10⁻⁴ N m), and unchanged by the fix of Section 5. This shift of resonant torque by about an orbit width is what KR2 claims for thermal ions at DIII-D's rational surfaces; for alphas it moves peaks rather than removing them.

4. The inner edge, and q₀

What goes wrong at λ = 1. With the published q (your Q18 B), ARC (q₀ = 0.89) and SPARC (q₀ = 0.93) start the response at q = 1.02, ψ = 0.343 and 0.327. The perturbation splines are extended below that with a cubic. Orbits launched near the cut, at the full width, reach below it and read the extension. At λ = 1 that dominates the ratio. With the extension replaced by zero, by its edge value (flat) or left cubic, the λ = 1 ratio is 0.40 / 0.88 / 1.69 in SPARC and 0.69 / 0.85 / 0.67 in ARC (Figure {{N_INNER}}). The physics gives no reason to prefer any of the three, so the λ = 1 points on the published q measure the extension, not the orbit average. From λ = 2 on, the affected band is under 0.02 in ψ and the ratios settle.

The q₀ > 1 check you allowed. You asked for approach A (q₀ > 1) against B (the cut) if cheap, and to go with A if it had a clear benefit. The extension sensitivity above is that benefit. Raising q₀ is exact and cheap: F² → F² + C changes neither ψ, the pressure nor FF′ and multiplies q by Fnew/Fold, so only the toroidal field changes, by a few percent at the axis. With q₀ = 1.1 (q₉₅ ≈ 3.95) the response starts at the axis and no orbit leaves it inward; the ARC λ = 1 ratio is 0.91. The surface torque does change: with q = 1 gone, the core alpha torque is set by the m = 1 response near q = 1, and it falls from 2.6×10⁻³ to 8.6×10⁻⁴ and 2.7×10⁻⁴ N m as q₀ goes 1.05, 1.1, 1.2 (Figure {{N_Q0}}). There is no plateau, so the absolute torque depends on the choice of q₀; the ratio KR1 plots depends on it much less (0.91 at 1.1). ITER's equilibrium already has q₀ = 1.30 and needs nothing. I built the q₀ = 1.1 families for ARC and SPARC and kept q₀ = 1.2 as a sensitivity point for ARC at λ = 1.

5. A defect in the energy refinement

The symptom. The first dense ARC λ = 16 run gave a ratio of 1.09, against 0.9997 at λ = 8. All of the excess came from one surface near the edge, ψ = 0.99275: forb was +2.7×10⁻³ N m there and −2.2×10⁻⁶ on a surface 3.5×10⁻⁶ away in ψ, with the same orbit counts. Taken apart, the excess was all in ℓ = 0, from a pitch band λ = 0.858–0.871 where the pitch integrand reached 2×10⁶ against ~50 elsewhere (Figure {{N_DEFECT}}b).

The cause. On each orbit segment (the energies at one pitch with the same orbit class), forb samples |δJℓ|², the bounce frequency and the precession, and adds midpoints wherever a cubic through every other node misses the rest by more than the tolerance. On pitch node 100 the data had a 2.5% step in |δJ|² at u = √(E/T) = 2.095. A step never passes that test, so every midpoint went to it: 35 of the segment's 72 nodes ended up within 10⁻⁴ in u. A cubic through nodes that close overshoots in the gap that follows, here to 7.8×10⁻³ against data no larger than 6×10⁻⁷ (Figure {{N_DEFECT}}a). I have not explained the step itself; the fix makes the interpolation robust to it.

The fix (gpec 93fe67df1, six lines in OrbitTorque.jl). Midpoints are no closer than 10⁻³ of the segment, the tolerance already used for class edges, and refinement stops when a pass adds no node. The spike surface now gives −2.23×10⁻⁶ and its neighbours are unchanged to four digits. The parked SPARC outlier from earlier in M4 (Im forb 27.1 against 7.8×10⁻³ on one surface) was the same defect: it is now 0.019. A scan of every run for surfaces more than 20 times their neighbours and fgar finds only these two, apart from the ℓ = −2 resonance of Section 3.

Mixed code. The fix went onto all three machines at 13:00, while the families were running. Surfaces computed before then keep the old code; the two flagged surfaces were rerun and replace their old values. To test that the old code is otherwise the same, the whole ARC λ = 8 profile (151 surfaces) was rerun with the fix: {{FIXCHK}}.

6. ITER and SPARC

ITER. No ITER gEQDSK with kinetic profiles was at hand, so I rebuilt OpenFUSIONToolkit's H-mode example (ITER_Hmode_ex) with TokaMaker and its bootstrap-current solve: 13 MA, q₀ = 1.30, q₉₅ = 3.48, βN = 1.98, with the example's own profiles. That is not the 15 MA Q = 10 baseline. Its last closed surface is truncated at ψ = 0.985.

SPARC. The SPARC public PRD equilibrium (FreeGS, 513²) with TRANSP profiles, ni = 0.85 ne. The two files disagree on the edge flux (TRANSP 2.338, gEQDSK 2.463 Wb/rad). Each is normalized to its own ψN, which puts the profiles 5% outward in flux if the gEQDSK is right. q₀ = 0.93 in GPEC (the gEQDSK's q column is zero).
