This is the third attempt at M2, which you granted in Q13 B. M2 adds forb to GPEC: the NTV torque computed along each particle's real guiding-center orbit instead of on its start flux surface. You asked for two things: close the two floors in the zero-orbit-width limit before M3 (Q11 B), and work out why trapped orbits stopped closing with the electrostatic potential on, keeping it on in the parity test with a 15-minute limit (Q12 B). Both questions were checked by independent review agents, as you asked.

In brief. Both floors and the closure failure came from the orbit code, not from the table or the physics. The orbit field rebuilt the flux-coordinate Jacobian from derivatives of R and Z, and that copy is rough near the edge. It caused the 1.5% precession offset (floor B) and, through the orbit's timing, the |δJ|² floor (A). The closure test keyed trapped and passing on reversals of v∥, but with the potential on two known orbit types do not reverse v∥ where it expects: they are E×B-modified orbits, not potato orbits. With those fixed, the zero-orbit-width limit falls with no floor in both variants: A as a clean power law of slope 0.98 over 3.5 decades, B by 4.8 decades. The physical-width torque converges within 2.7% with the potential off or on. The parity suite runs in 4 min 40 s, and tables build 4.3 times faster. M2 is green. One item that physics.md placed in M2, the phase-space measure, has not been done and is proposed as the first task of M3 (Q14).

1. What attempt 3 changed, and how it was checked

The object. For each flux surface ψ₀ (ψ the normalized poloidal flux), forb launches guiding-center orbits from the point of minimum field strength θbmin on that surface. Each orbit is labelled by its pitch λ = μB₀/E (μ the magnetic moment, E the kinetic energy, B₀ a reference field), its normalized speed u = √(E/T) (T the temperature) and the sign σ of its parallel velocity v∥. The orbit's bounce frequency ωb, mean toroidal rate ⟨φ̇⟩ and the bounce harmonics |δJℓ|² of the perturbed action go into the same resonant pitch and energy integrals that the surface method fgar uses. The resonance is Ωℓ = ℓωb − n⟨φ̇⟩, with ℓ an integer bounce harmonic. Attempt 2 built that per-column table and its integrals. Attempt 3 changes only how a single orbit is integrated and classified (Figure {{N_CLOSURE}}).

The three changes (gpec 1463259f5, all in Orbits.jl):

  1. Closure by topology. E, μ and the canonical toroidal momentum Pφ are conserved, so at each (ψ, θ) they fix v∥, and every orbit is a closed curve on the cylinder (ψ, θ mod 1). An orbit closes the first time it crosses θ ≡ θ₀ (mod 1) in its starting direction at ψ = ψ₀. Its net winding k is the class: k = 0 trapped, k = ±1 passing. The old test asked for two v∥ reversals (trapped) or none (passing) and failed every orbit for which v∥ and θ̇ reverse at different times. The count of start-section crossings per period is also recorded: 2 for a trapped orbit and 1 for a passing one. That count is what the ½ and 1 weights in the pitch integral assume.
  2. Jacobian from the equilibrium. The orbit field used 𝒥 = R(RθZψ − RψZθ) built from ψ-derivatives of the R, Z map. The surface path uses the equilibrium's own rzphi_jac. On average the two agree, but the rebuilt one is rough in θ near the edge (Section 3). The orbit field now uses rzphi_jac.
  3. Precession without cancellation. A trapped orbit's precession was the small remainder of two large toroidal legs, so its error grew as Z × the ODE tolerance. The orbit now integrates χ = φ − ∫(Bφ/Bθ)(ψ₀, θ)dθ, with the parallel streaming cancelled analytically inside the right-hand side. ⟨φ̇⟩ = (Δχ + 2πqk)/τ is the same quantity, now accurate to the ODE tolerance at any width. A unit test checks it at Z = 4096 for tolerances 10⁻⁸ and 10⁻¹¹.

How it was checked. As you asked, three review agents worked from the code, physics.md, the attempt-2 numbers and your words, without my hypotheses. One took the per-orbit numerics against fgar, one the table architecture, and one the orbit classes with a radial electric field (reports in results/m2a3_dev/review_*.md). While they worked I ran three probes: orbit portraits with the potential on, a field-by-field comparison of the orbit and surface inputs, and a Jacobian swap. The reviewers and the probes reached the same two causes independently. The table reviewer recommended keeping the per-column table: its pitch coordinate already straightens the class boundaries, and it found no way for the table to make a floor that does not change with Z. The swap tests had already pointed the same way, since the floors left when a single orbit quantity was replaced.

2. Orbits with the potential on (your Q12 question)

What failed. With the electrostatic potential Φ in the orbit Hamiltonian (your Q8 default), many orbits never met the closure test and ran to the time limit: 21 of 144 in a DIII-D probe at ψ = 0.5 and 40 of 144 in TJ. I integrated each one for a long time and recorded energy conservation, reversals of v∥ and of θ̇, and the net poloidal winding (probe P3, phi_orbits.jl). Energy holds to 10⁻¹⁴–10⁻⁷ on every one, so the integrator is not at fault. The orbits fall into two types (Figure {{N_PHI}}).

Not potato orbits. A potato orbit is a banana orbit wide enough to enclose the magnetic axis. That needs the orbit width to be comparable to the distance from the axis. The potato width (2q²ρ²R)1/3 (Helander 2000, cited from the classes review, which read it in full; ρ the Larmor radius) is 6–8 cm for thermal deuterium here, against 0.4–0.7 m from the axis at ψ = 0.5. The orbits in Figure {{N_PHI}} move by less than 0.03 in ψ. Potatoes do matter for the M3 alphas near the axis: the review estimates r/a ≲ 0.15–0.2 for 3.5 MeV alphas in ITER and SPARC. The new closure test handles them, because they wind like any other closed curve.

What they are. These are the two orbit types of a strong radial electric field (Shaing's E×B-modified trapping, via Trinczek 2023, which the review read in full).

  • Trapped by E×B (Figure {{N_PHI}}a, b). The poloidal motion v∥b·∇θ + vE·∇θ reverses, but v∥ does not, because the trapping region in v∥ is centred on −RωE instead of zero. vE is the E×B drift and ωE the E×B rotation frequency. The measured orbit-averaged v∥ is the same at every energy and pitch and equals the input RωE: 0.19vt against 0.19 in DIII-D, and 1.0–1.1vt against 0.97 in TJ (vt = √(2T/m) the thermal speed). Every unclosed DIII-D orbit and 37 of the 40 in TJ are of this type.
  • Carried round by E×B (Figure {{N_PHI}}c). These are magnetically trapped, so v∥ oscillates through zero, but the E×B drift carries them round in θ. 3 of the 40 in TJ.

TJ rotates at the thermal speed. The TJ deck sets ωE = 2×10⁴(1 − ψ) rad/s with Ti = 20 + 180(1 − ψ) eV on R₀ = 10 m. At ψ = 0.5 that gives RωE = 1.0×10⁵ m/s against vt = 1.03×10⁵ m/s. Most thermal ions below u ≈ 1 are then E×B-dominated, which is outside the drift-kinetic ordering physics.md §4 assumes. That sentence reads "E×B speed small against the thermal speed". The E×B speed itself is small here (vE/vt ≈ 0.04); what sets the orbit topology is RωE, which is vE divided by Bp/B. So TJ with Φ on serves the classifier as a stress test, not as a zero-orbit-width comparison. The DIII-D-like case, at RωE/vt ≈ 0.2, is inside the ordering and is the one on KR2's path. Q14 proposes restating physics.md §4 in terms of RωE.

Cost and the parity test. Unclosed orbits ran to 11 times the time limit and were then thrown away. The table reviewer estimated they took 65% of the Z = 1 table time even with Φ off, mostly near the axis, where wide orbits wind with v∥ reversals. With them closing, the Z = 1 table of 54 surfaces costs 1.2 h of CPU (attempt 2: 5.1 h), and the whole solve-API suite with the parity test runs in 4 min 40 s, inside your 15-minute limit. Near the axis (ψ = 0.05 and 0.10, Z = 1, Φ off, u up to 6.5) every trapped orbit crosses the start section twice per period and every passing orbit once, so the ½ and 1 weights hold there.

3. The zero-orbit-width limit (your Q11 question)

The check. Raising the charge Z shrinks the orbit width Δb ∝ 1/Z, so forb should approach the replay, the surface values from fgar pushed through the same orbit integrals on the same pitch grid. Variant A raises Z only. Variant B also divides ωE and the collision frequency ν by Z, which keeps the resonances sharp. Attempt 2 found two floors that did not move with Z. Swapping one orbit quantity at a time for its surface value traced A to the trapped |δJℓ|² at the edge and B to a 1.5% offset in trapped precession at ψ ≈ 0.92, where ωE crosses zero.

Cause of both: the rebuilt Jacobian. Probe P2 compared every orbit-field input with the surface path's source on a θ grid. |B| and ∂B/∂ψ are identical, because both come from the same spline. The rebuilt Jacobian equals the equilibrium's on average, but it is rough in θ. Its second differences are 10⁻⁵ of its size at ψ = 0.5 and 10⁻² from ψ = 0.92 out, against 10⁻¹² for rzphi_jac. Its ψ-derivative is off by up to 75% at ψ = 0.92 (Figure {{N_JAC}}). It enters the curvature terms of the orbit drift (precession, floor B) and dt along the orbit (timing and phase in δJ, floor A). Using rzphi_jac moved the median trapped precession, orbit over surface, at ψ = 0.918 from 0.9858 to 0.99999, with ψ = 0.5 unchanged (job 139296). The numerics reviewer named the same cause independently. It also found that the precession offset was up to 4.5% at some pitches, and that it returned towards 1 for the deepest orbits, where the roughness is smallest: the pattern of noise, not of a physics term.

The scan after the fixes. I first reran the scan exactly as in attempt 2 (array 139359). A now falls with no floor. B falls from 3.9×10⁻² to 1.1×10⁻⁵ and levels at 6.5×10⁻⁶. That level sits at the ωE = 0 crossing (ψ = 0.918–0.932), is the same at every Z, and is trapped precession again (swap: 8.6×10⁻⁵ → 2.4×10⁻⁶ per surface). Tightening the quadrature tolerances 10× and 100× changes nothing. Raising the θ resolution of the surface reference does: fgar's default of 128 θ points gives 8.6×10⁻⁵ at ψ = 0.921, and 256, 512 and 1024 points give 5.5×10⁻⁶, 1.3×10⁻⁶ and 1.0×10⁻⁶ (job 139406). So the last level was the reference's error, and the orbit was the more accurate side. Doubling the orbit's δJ samples from 256 to 1024 changes nothing (jobs 139302–3). Against a replay built at 512 θ points (array 139407), both variants fall with no floor (Figure {{N_ZOW}}).

Variant B is not a single power law. It drops 68 times between Z = 256 and Z = 1024, then falls with slope about 1, and about 0.5 in the last step. Figure {{N_FLOOR}}b shows the drop happens in the edge surfaces. My unconfirmed explanation: in variant B every frequency except ωb scales as 1/Z, so the ℓ ≠ 0 bounce resonances move to energies that grow with Z and leave the energy range (u ≤ 6.5) near Z ≈ 500. From there only the ℓ = 0 resonance is left, whose position does not move. I have not tested this. The check as restated asks for a power law with no floor. A meets that as written; B has no floor over 4.8 decades but is not a single power law.

Two things for develop, not for this project (parked in JOURNAL). fgar's default 128 θ points leave a trapped-precession error of about 10⁻⁴ near an ωE = 0 crossing, and its default 128 pitch nodes leave a 2.5% torque-density error at ψ = 0.7 (attempt 2). Both belong in one issue for develop.

4. Physical width: convergence, cost and the torque

Convergence. At Z = 1 I doubled the pitch nodes and halved the energy tolerance on three surfaces, with Φ off and on (Figure {{N_CONV}}). From 64 to 128 nodes the complex torque moves by at most 2.7% with Φ off and 2.6% with Φ on, inside the 5% check. Tables at 64 nodes take 1.0–1.5 min per surface with Φ off and 1.4–1.8 min with Φ on, about half of attempt 2's time. The Φ-off torques are unchanged from attempt 2 (0.0371 + 0.0818i at ψ = 0.5).

The torque at physical width. Figure {{N_PROFILE}} shows the Z = 1 torque density of thermal deuterium in the DIII-D-like case with Φ on, the default since Q8, beside Φ off and fgar. Integrated over ψ: fgar 0.536 + 0.169i N·m; forb 0.204 − 0.036i with Φ off and 0.119 + 0.077i with Φ on. Inside ψ = 0.9 the three nearly agree in real part (0.070, 0.059, 0.059). Above it fgar has 0.466, forb 0.145 with Φ off and 0.060 with Φ on. With Φ on, the peak at the ωE = 0 crossing (ψ ≈ 0.92) is gone and the edge peak is lower and broader. That is the mechanism physics.md §4 names for KR2: an orbit that straddles the ωE zero sees its average. With Φ on the table costs 1.25 h of CPU and no orbit is left unclosed. One sign of what the table reviewer flagged: 2969 sampled nodes with Φ on (606 with Φ off), across 12,332 segments, fall in a class other than their segment's and are dropped. These are thin class bands that the 24-point scan in u misses; the class-edge change in Section 5 would catch them.

Not done: the phase-space measure. physics.md §3 gives the orbit path's phase-space measure as Gorelenkov 1999 Eq. 22, "check the denominator against the PDF in M2". It is not in the code. The pitch integral uses fgar's measure, so the Jacobian |∂Pφ/∂ψ₀| at fixed (E, μ, θ₀), which relates a start label ψ₀ to the constants of motion, is missing. It goes to 1 as the orbit width goes to zero, so it cannot touch check 1. The table reviewer estimates it at about 1% for thermal ions at Z = 1 and up to tens of percent for alphas with Δb/a ≈ 0.1–0.3. The Z = 1 profile above lacks it. It belongs with the M3 drive ∂f/∂Pφ, which needs the same derivative, so Q14 proposes moving it there as M3's first task.

Q14 - physics.md wording after attempt 3, and moving the measure to M3 (gates M3)

Found. The code now differs from physics.md in three places, each for a reason given above, and one M2 item is not done. (a) §2 says the Jacobian comes from R, Z; the code uses rzphi_jac, because the rebuilt one is rough near the edge (Section 3). (b) §2 classifies orbits by v∥ reversals; the code closes on the first return in the start direction and classifies by net poloidal winding, which covers the two E×B orbit types, potatoes and stagnation orbits (Section 2). (c) §4 states the ordering as "E×B speed small against the thermal speed"; what sets the orbit topology is RωE ≪ vt, and TJ with Φ on violates it (Section 2). (d) The §3 measure, assigned to M2, is not in the code (Section 4).

Why it gates M3. M3's alphas add potato and stagnation orbits to the same classifier, and their weight and measure follow from it, so physics.md should say what the code does before M3 builds on it.

5. What the reviews proposed that I have not done

You asked whether a review could find methods that are both more stable and faster. The stability part is in Sections 2 and 3. On speed, the closure fix gave the largest gain, 4.3× on the Z = 1 table. The table reviewer proposed three more changes. Its estimate is that together they would take the Z = 1 profile from 1.2 h to well under an hour of table time and cut the integral stage by about 3×; I have not measured that.

  • Class edges without orbits. Each pitch column scans 24 orbits in u and bisects the class changes. Most of those probes are discarded, and a class band thinner than the 0.28 scan spacing can be missed. The class boundaries are where the constants-of-motion function has critical values on the curves ∂B/∂θ = 0, so they can be computed from the field alone, leaving one orbit per segment to confirm the class.
  • Refinement weighted by energy. 49 of 149 segments hit the 64-node cap in a Z = 1 table, and 31% of their nodes sit at u > 4, where the Maxwellian weight is negligible. The refinement test can weight each quantity by its contribution to the energy integral and treat each ℓ separately.
  • The integral stage. It rebuilds a spline at every pitch evaluation and runs two small eigenvalue solves in every spline cell, which can be cached per group.

The numerics reviewer's main speed lever was passing interpolation hints to the orbit right-hand side (its estimate 2–4× per orbit), also not done. None of these changes the answer, so they can wait.
