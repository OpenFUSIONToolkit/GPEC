The zero-orbit-width limit works: the orbit torque converges to the surface torque with no floor. At physical width the result is not determined. The boundary between trapped and passing orbits is handled by filling a layer with surface values, and that layer covers most of the table at high energy, so a parameter with no physical value moves the Z = 1 torque by −29% to +39%. Attempt 2 has to replace the layer. Card Q9 sets its scope and what green means. Attempt 2 is the last M2 attempt under the stop rules, so I stop here for the answer.

Question

When the action variation δJ is computed along the real guiding-center orbit (M1) instead of on a flux surface, and fed through today's resonance operator and energy integral with the orbit's own bounce and toroidal frequencies, does the torque go back to today's surface-local (zero-orbit-width, ZOW) torque as the orbit width goes to zero? How fast? And is the finite-width answer converged?

What changed

A new method forb in KineticForces, in a new file OrbitTorque.jl. For each ψ surface it tabulates, on the existing pitch grid λ and on 12 energy nodes in √x and both signs of v∥, the orbit's ωb, its toroidal rate ⟨φ̇⟩, and |δJℓ|² for ℓ = −L…L. δJℓ integrates the same bracket BounceAveraging uses (∇·ξ⊥ and δB/B, read at the orbit's (ψ, θ)) with the perturbation's toroidal phase e−in(2πζ − Δζ τ/τb) along the orbit, where ζ is GPEC's toroidal angle (φ = 2πζ + ν). The table goes into the existing resonance operator and energy integral, splined in √x, with the same pole handling as fgar. Lost orbits count zero. Unclosed orbits, orbits whose class differs from the surface's at that pitch, and pitches within a layer around the trapped–passing boundary take the surface value. The layer's half-width is 10× the bisected shift of the orbit's own boundary, set per energy node. New deck keys: forb_flag, orbit_nlambda (128), orbit_nenergy (12), each with a deck/API parity case. Out of scope here, as planned: the drive is still ∂f/∂ψ at the start surface ψ₀, and the measure is the ZOW one (dPφ ≈ Ze dψ₀). M3 replaces both.

Figures

Reading

The orbit path converges to the surface path, with no floor. The difference falls from 4.5% at Δb/a = 3×10−3 to 6×10−5 at 10−5. There is no plateau that a wrong phase, Jacobian or sign would leave. The same orbit table, with surface values put in, reproduces fgar to roundoff. The M1 sign map (⟨φ̇⟩ = s·ωDx) and the toroidal phase convention (ζ, with ν) are confirmed against BounceAveraging for every ℓ. All of this is on the s = −1 sign of Bθ (both test equilibria have it); s = +1 is first exercised on ARC in M4.

At physical width, the boundary layer decides the answer. At finite width the orbit's trapped–passing boundary moves off λ = B₀/Bmax by an amount that grows with energy and differs between the two signs of v∥. Near it, |δJ|² changes like 1/(λ − λc), and on the fixed λ grid this gave a 10−3 floor that did not fall with width. The fill I chose (surface values within 10× the bisected shift) removes the floor, and it disappears at small width as intended. At Z = 16 the layer factor changes the answer by under 0.4 points out of 5.7%. At Z = 1 it does not work. On the edge surface ψN = 0.96, 34% of all table entries are lost orbits, mostly at high energy. For √x ≥ 2.4, 80–95% of entries hold no orbit value (lost, unclosed, class-mismatched or in the layer). The layer factor alone moves that share at the top energy node from 76% (factor 3) to 94% (10) to 100% (30). The fill also writes surface values over lost orbits, which otherwise count zero. So the factor mixes two things: how much of the boundary region is surface-valued, and whether lost orbits stay at zero. The physical-width forb torque follows it: 0.26, 0.36 and 0.50 N·m for factors 3, 10 and 30, with fgar at 0.53. Doubling λ cannot detect this. The λ grid is fgar's, clustered quadratically at λtp (spacing 10−5 there against a median of 10−2), not at the orbit's own boundary λc(E, σ), so doubling it refines the wrong place. What attempt 2 does (inference, not yet tested): drop the fill and tabulate on a per-class pitch coordinate normalized to the bisected boundary, t = (λ − λc)/(λmax − λc) for trapped and λ/λc for passing. The table then lives on a fixed (t, √x) grid, the energy spline at fixed t is smooth, and λc enters only through dλ/dt. Lost orbits stay at zero (until M3's loss boundary replaces that). The existing energy integral and pole handling are unchanged. Green would mean the layer factor is gone, and the Z = 1 torque changes by < 5% on doubling the t grid.

Why the convergence is first order, not second. M1 showed the frequency errors are odd in the sign of v∥, so they cancel to second order when both signs are summed. A first-order residual needs a first-order source, and M2 has four by design: (i) the drive ∂f/∂ψ at ψ₀, not at the orbit's mean ψ; (ii) the measure dPφ ≈ Ze dψ₀; (iii) lost orbits set to zero, whose share of the table falls with width (24% of entries at Z = 1, 5.5% at Z = 16, 0.2% at Z = 1024, mostly on the edge surfaces where the torque is); (iv) the boundary layer. The Z = 16 layer run rules out (iv) as the source of the slope. Which of (i)–(iii) dominates is untested. I infer that (i) and (ii) set it, and the test is to repeat the scan after M3, when both are exact. A slope near 2 there would mean the orbit average itself is second order, as Liu 2014 finds for the precession resonance. A slope near 1 would mean a real first-order effect, such as the passing-ion label shift M1 found. Either is a result for the paper.

Two details of the scan. Z changes the resonances as well as the width (ωD and ω* scale as 1/Z), so the ZOW reference moves: Re Tfgar = +0.554, +0.012, −0.112, −0.151 N·m at Z = 1, 4, 16, 64. Its zero crossing makes the Z = 4 relative difference meaningless as a convergence point. Also, the relative difference depends on the ψ nodes. On 36 nodes instead of 72 it is 5.7% instead of 4.5% at Z = 16, because the difference lives in narrow edge features. Each comparison uses common nodes, so the slope stands, but the absolute values in Figure 1 carry about this much ψ-quadrature uncertainty. The KR3 version of the figure should use more nodes at the edge.

Cost. The orbit tables take about 130 core-seconds per ψ surface, 180 at Z = 1. That is 15–20× M1's 10-minute estimate for the whole grid. Per orbit it is 40–60 ms, against M1's 3 ms for the orbit alone, so δJ sampling (256 points × 13 harmonics × all m) dominates the table. On 16 threads, Z ≥ 4 runs at 73% efficiency. Z = 1 runs at 27%, because a few edge surfaces with many lost and unclosed orbits take longest. The solve-API parity case is the bigger problem. On the TJ deck it takes 22 min per run on 2 threads, against 45 s without forb. A profile of one surface puts the table at 5.7 s, of which 1.2 s is orbits. So nearly all the time is in the energy and pitch integrals of the table: each pitch evaluation rebuilds the √x spline and scans 401 points for poles. A test that slow cannot stay in the suite. Making these integrals fast belongs in attempt 2 either way, so it is in Next, not a card.

Decisions

Q9 - Scope of M2 attempt 2, and what green means (gates attempt 2)

What was found. Two things. (1) The ZOW limit converges as a power law with no floor, at slope 1.2–1.4, so PLAN's "10−4 at Δb/a < 10−3" fails by 80×. Three of M2's first-order simplifications (drive at ψ₀, ZOW measure, lost orbits zeroed) are M3's to remove. (2) At physical width the trapped–passing boundary layer sets the torque to ±40% (check 4). That is a numerical defect in M2 itself and needs a fix (Reading).

Why this is yours to decide. Attempt 2 is the last M2 attempt: if it ends not green, the project stops. The 10−4 target is in PLAN, and the project rules don't let me redefine a check after seeing the numbers.

Green for attempt 2: no layer parameter left; the Z = 1 torque changes by < 5% on doubling the pitch and energy grids; the scan still shows a power law with no floor; the parity test runs in under 2 min. M3's "ZOW limit still holds" check becomes the same scan on the M3 code, and its slope is reported.
This pulls most of M3's measure work into M2's last attempt. If the true orbit average is first order (the passing-ion label shift), 10−4 still fails and the project stops.
M2 is green now with a known open defect. Every M3 check that compares against M2 ("thermal torque sign unchanged") would then inherit a ±40% baseline at physical width.
Recommendation: A, fairly sure. The power law with no floor is what catches a wrong phase, Jacobian or sign, which is check 1's purpose, and the 10−4 number assumed a second-order difference that M2's planned simplifications rule out. The layer is M2's own defect and belongs in M2. The fix keeps the energy integral and the operator as they are. Gating: attempt 2 waits for this answer.

Terms: ZOW - zero orbit width; Δb/a - orbit width over minor radius; ψ₀ - the surface the orbit starts on; ⟨ψ⟩ - the orbit's mean ψ; slope - exponent p in difference ∝ (Δb/a)p; λc - the orbit's own trapped–passing pitch at a given energy and sign of v∥; layer - pitches near λc given surface values.

Q10 - δWK as a scalar on the orbit path (not gating)

What was found. Today's path builds an m×m′ δWK matrix on each surface from ξ on that surface. An orbit started at ψ₀ reads ξ over its whole radial excursion, so its δWK is a kernel coupling ψ and ψ′, with no per-surface matrix form. The kinetic ForceFreeStates ODE needs a local matrix, so the orbit path cannot feed it. PLAN's KR3 figure ("the dominant δWK matrix element") and checks 2–3 assume it can. What the path does give is the total kinetic energy δWK = Im T/2n and the torque, for a given perturbation.

Why this is yours to decide. It changes the KR3 figure's y axis in PLAN, and it keeps the orbit path to post-response torque and energy, with no self-consistent kinetic response.

Matches what the Letter needs, since both key results are torques on a given perturbation. PLAN's KR3 figure text changes; no new work.
Opens self-consistent finite-orbit kinetic response, but needs a non-local ODE closure that GPEC does not have. That is a project on its own, beyond the 8-milestone budget.
Recommendation: A, sure. B is a separate project (a Feature Add idea, not this Letter). Not gating.

Terms: δWK - kinetic energy of the perturbation; T = Tφ + i·2nδWK - GPEC's complex torque; m, m′ - poloidal harmonics; KR3 - the methods result (ZOW limit).
