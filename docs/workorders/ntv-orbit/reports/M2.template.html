Question

When the action variation δJ is computed along the real guiding-center orbit (M1) instead of on a flux surface, and fed through today's resonance operator and energy integral with the orbit's own bounce and toroidal frequencies, does the torque go back to today's surface-local (zero-orbit-width, ZOW) torque as the orbit width goes to zero? And how fast?

What changed

A new method forb in KineticForces, in a new file OrbitTorque.jl. For each ψ surface it tabulates, on the existing pitch grid λ and on 12 energy nodes in √x and both signs of v∥, the orbit's ωb, its toroidal rate ⟨φ̇⟩, and |δJℓ|² for ℓ = −L…L. δJℓ integrates the same bracket BounceAveraging uses (∇·ξ⊥ and δB/B, read at the orbit's (ψ, θ)) with the perturbation's toroidal phase e−in(2πζ − Δζ τ/τb) along the orbit, where ζ is GPEC's toroidal angle (φ = 2πζ + ν). The table goes into the existing resonance operator and energy integral, splined in √x, with the same pole handling as fgar. Orbits that leave the plasma (lost) count zero. Unclosed orbits, and orbits of the other class (trapped vs passing) near the boundary, take the surface value. So do pitches within a thin layer around the trapped–passing boundary, whose width is set per energy from the bisected orbit boundary. New deck keys: forb_flag, orbit_nlambda (128), orbit_nenergy (12), each with a deck/API parity case. Out of scope here, as planned: the drive is still ∂f/∂ψ at the start surface ψ₀, and the measure is the ZOW one (dPφ ≈ Ze dψ₀). M3 replaces both.

Figures

Reading

The orbit path converges to the surface path, with no floor. The difference falls from 4.5% at Δb/a = 3×10−3 to 6×10−5 at 10−5. There is no plateau that a wrong phase, Jacobian or sign would leave. The same orbit table, with surface values put in, reproduces fgar to roundoff. The sign map from M1 (⟨φ̇⟩ = s·ωDx) and the toroidal phase convention (ζ, with ν) are confirmed against BounceAveraging for every ℓ. This is all on the s = −1 sign of Bθ (both test equilibria have it); s = +1 is first exercised on ARC in M4.

Why first order, and why that is expected in M2. M1 showed the frequency errors are odd in the sign of v∥, so they cancel to second order in the sum over both signs. A first-order residual needs a first-order source. M2 has four, all by design: (i) the drive ∂f/∂ψ is taken at ψ₀, not at the orbit's mean ψ; (ii) the measure is dPφ ≈ Ze dψ₀; (iii) lost orbits count zero, and the lost fraction of the table scales with width (24% of table entries at Z = 1, 5.5% at Z = 16, 0.2% at Z = 1024; most are on the edge surfaces where the torque is); (iv) the separatrix layer takes surface values over a width proportional to the orbit width. Check 4's layer variants bound (iv) at Z = 1. Which of (i)–(iii) dominates is untested. It is my inference that (i) and (ii) together set the slope, and the test is the same scan after M3, when both become exact. If the slope then rises to about 2, the orbit average itself is second order, as Liu 2014 has it for the precession resonance. If it stays near 1, the passing-ion label shift from M1 is a real first-order effect. Either is a paper result. So card Q9 asks to state the target as a power law and to re-measure it at M3, not to lower it permanently.

The Z = 1 to Z = 4 points. Z changes the resonances as well as the width (ωD and ω* scale as 1/Z), so the ZOW reference moves: Re Tfgar = +0.554, +0.012, −0.112, −0.151 N·m at Z = 1, 4, 16, 64. Its zero crossing makes the Z = 4 relative difference meaningless as a convergence point, which is why the fit starts at Z = 16. At Z = 4 the torque agrees to 4% while the complex T differs by 37%: the energy part converges more slowly. My reading, not tested, is that the energy is non-resonant and so is weighted toward the top of the energy grid, where orbits are widest. The scan is a mass-free charge scan, not an energy scan; KR1's energy scan in M4 is the physical version.

At physical width (Z = 1) forb is 33% below fgar in torque (0.371 vs 0.554 N·m). This is not yet a physics result. It is shaped by the lost-orbit zeroing (24% of table entries) and the drive at ψ₀, which M3 replaces with the loss boundary and ∂f/∂Pφ. Figure 2 shows where the difference is: the edge peaks at ψN = 0.92, 0.96 and 0.993 are halved or removed, which is the direction KR2 predicts.

The trapped–passing boundary layer. At finite width the orbit's trapped–passing boundary moves off λ = B₀/Bmax by an amount that differs per energy and per sign of v∥. On a fixed λ grid, |δJ|² near the boundary changes like 1/(λ − λtp), and the pitch integral then had a 10−3 floor that did not fall with width. The fix bisects the actual orbit boundary per energy and takes surface values within 10× that shift of λtp. The layer is first order in width, so it goes away in the limit. It is the main numerical choice in this path that M3's boundary work could refine.

Cost. M1 estimated 10 single-threaded minutes for the M2 orbit grid. Measured: the orbit tables take about 130 core-seconds per ψ surface (180 at Z = 1), so 9,300–12,900 core-seconds per 72-surface scan, 15–20× the estimate. Per orbit that is about 40–60 ms against M1's 3 ms for the orbit alone, so δJ sampling (256 points × 13 harmonics) dominates. On 16 threads, Z ≥ 4 runs at 73% efficiency (13 min). Z = 1 runs at 27% (50 min), limited by a few edge surfaces whose many lost and unclosed orbits take longest. The solve-API parity case adds {{PARITY_TIME}}. This is the expected PLAN M2 cost card (Q11).

Decisions

Q9 - The ZOW-limit target for check 1 (gates M3)

What was found. The orbit torque converges to the surface torque as a power law with no floor (Figure 1), but at slope ≈ 1.2–1.4, not 2. At Δb/a = 7×10−4 the difference is 8×10−3, so PLAN's "10−4 at Δb/a < 10−3" fails by 80×. M2 has four first-order pieces by design: drive at ψ₀, the ZOW measure, lost orbits zeroed, and the boundary layer (Reading). M3 makes the first three exact.

Why this is yours to decide. The target is in PLAN, and its first-order failure decides whether M2 is green. The project rules don't let me redefine a check after seeing the numbers.

M2 is green now, and M3 starts. M3's check "the ZOW limit still holds with the new drive" becomes the same scan, run on the new code. The slope after M3 is reported either way: about 2 would mean the orbit average is second order, about 1 would mean a real first-order passing-ion effect.
Attempt 2 would build the drive at ⟨ψ⟩ and the exact Jacobian now. That is most of M3's code, done in M2 to meet M2's check, and spends 1 of the 2 M2 attempts. A first-order passing-ion effect would still fail it.
Saves one scan (about 1.5 h on 16 CPUs). But it loses the cleanest test of whether the first order is numerical or physical.
Recommendation: A, fairly sure. The power law with no floor is the property that catches a wrong phase, Jacobian or sign, and that is what check 1 is for. The 10−4 number assumed second order, which M2's planned simplifications rule out. Gating: M3 waits for this answer, since its own ZOW check is defined by it.

Terms: ZOW - zero orbit width; Δb/a - orbit width over minor radius; ψ₀ - the surface the orbit starts on; Pφ - canonical toroidal momentum; slope - exponent p in difference ∝ (Δb/a)p.

Q10 - δWK as a scalar on the orbit path (not gating)

What was found. Today's path builds an m×m′ δWK matrix on each surface, from ξ on that surface. An orbit started at ψ₀ reads ξ over its whole radial excursion, so its δWK is a kernel coupling ψ and ψ′. That has no per-surface matrix form. The kinetic ForceFreeStates ODE needs a local matrix, so the orbit path cannot feed it. PLAN's KR3 figure ("the dominant δWK matrix element") and checks 2–3 assume it can. What the path does give is the total kinetic energy δWK = Im T/2n and the torque, for a given perturbation.

Why this is yours to decide. It changes the KR3 figure's y axis in PLAN, and it limits the orbit path to post-response torque and energy, with no self-consistent kinetic response.

Matches what the Letter needs: both key results are torques on a given perturbation. PLAN's KR3 figure text changes; no new work.
Opens self-consistent finite-orbit kinetic response, but needs a non-local ODE closure that GPEC does not have. That is a project on its own, beyond the 8-milestone budget.
Recommendation: A, sure. B is a separate project (a Feature Add idea, not this Letter). Not gating: M3 needs neither form.

Terms: δWK - kinetic energy of the perturbation; T = Tφ + i·2nδWK - GPEC's complex torque; m, m′ - poloidal harmonics; KR3 - the methods result (ZOW limit).
