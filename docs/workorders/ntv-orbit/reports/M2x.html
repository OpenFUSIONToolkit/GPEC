Answers to three questions on the M2 report: what Z is and why the scan goes to Z = 4096; what the grids and the layer fill are; and what the proposed fixes change. One new finding: the attempt-2 plan as written in Q9 option A (clustering at the orbit's trapped–passing edge λc) is not enough at the edge, because for one sign of v∥ that edge does not exist above u ≈ 1. The grid has to cluster at every class edge, lost ones included (section 4).

1. What Z is

Z is GPEC's species charge number zi. In the scan, the particle is a deuteron (mass 2 mp) with charge Z·e. The temperature, density, collision frequency ν and E×B rotation ωE are read from the same profiles at every Z. Only Z = 1 is a physical plasma. Z > 1 is a numerical device: it makes the orbit narrower without touching the equilibrium, the perturbation or the ψ grid, so the zero-orbit-width (ZOW) limit can be approached in a controlled way.

Why it goes so high. The physical width at ψ = 0.5 is 4.7% of the minor radius. Each factor of 4 in Z divides it by 4, so Z = 4096 gives Δb/a = 1.1×10⁻⁵. Seven points across 3.5 decades are what it takes to tell a power law from a floor. A wrong phase, Jacobian or sign leaves a floor; the scan shows none.

What that means for the test. At each Z both paths, forb and fgar, see the same species, so the comparison is fair point by point. But the reference changes along the scan, so the slope assumes the error's prefactor does not change much with Z. The local slopes between neighbouring points show no drift:

Mass or temperature scans also move ωb, so the code has no knob that narrows the orbit and changes nothing else. A cleaner scan would also divide ωE and ν by Z, so every low frequency keeps its ratio to ωD and the reference stays nearly fixed. That is my inference, not tested. It would be a script-only change for attempt 2 if you want it.

2. The three grids

The orbit table at each ψ holds the orbit bounce frequency, precession and |δJℓ|² on a grid of pitch λ × energy u × sign of v∥. The pitch and energy integrals are then done on this table. The ψ integral is done over surfaces.

3. The layer fill

Each table entry is filled from one orbit started at (ψ, θBmin), using the rules in the left diagram. When the orbit is trapped or passing, like the surface class at that pitch, it supplies the value. A lost orbit, one that leaves the plasma, counts zero. An unclosed orbit, or one whose class differs from the surface class, takes the surface value. Then a last pass, the layer fill, overwrites every entry within 10 × |λc − λtp| of λtp with the surface value. Here λc is where the orbit itself turns from passing to trapped, found by bisection on each energy row. The layer was there to avoid the poorly resolved region between the two boundaries.

Even at the lowest energy the layer covers 14% of the nodes, because the grid packs nodes at λtp. Of the 3072 table entries on this surface, 1040 (34%) are lost orbits.

Reading. The evidence is that, on this surface, the layer pastes values 3–8 times larger than the orbit's own over most of the pitch range, and over the lost zeros. That fits the direction seen in M2: the Z = 1 torque moves toward fgar (0.26 → 0.36 → 0.50 N·m, against 0.53) as the layer widens. The inference, from one surface and two rows, is that the true Z = 1 forb torque at M2's level of physics is below the layer-10 value. It does not say by how much. The integrand near λc is smooth enough that clustered nodes resolve it, so no layer is needed there.

4. The proposed fixes

The fixes, as attempt 2 would make them:

  1. Pitch grid from the orbit's class edges (Figure 6b). This replaces the layer. Lost orbits stay zero. The Q9 text said "cluster at λc"; Figure 4a shows that is not enough, since for σ = +1 the edges that matter are passing–lost and trapped–lost.
  2. Energy integral done once per pitch node (Figure 3b). Today the pitch quadrature rebuilds the energy spline and re-scans for poles at every λ it evaluates, which is why the parity test takes 22 min. This is a cost fix, not a physics one.
  3. Rerun check 4 at Z = 1 and the scan with more edge ψ nodes, as in the M2 report.

Open design question, not settled by these figures. Per-row pitch grids do not line up across energy rows, but the energy integral needs values at a fixed pitch coordinate across rows. The fix is a per-class coordinate t ∈ (0, 1) between the row's edges, with nodes at fixed t. That works while each class is one interval whose ends move smoothly with u. Figure 4a breaks this: on σ = +1 the passing class vanishes between u = 1.4 and 1.9, so at fixed t the energy integrand has a step. The energy integral then also needs the u at which each class ends, found by bisection like λc. I would settle this as the first step of attempt 2, by plotting the integrand against u at fixed t on this surface, before writing the code.
