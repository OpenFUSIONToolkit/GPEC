This report carries the background, the pipeline, the current-behavior figure (the "before" of key result 2) and the three decisions that gate milestone M1.

Background

GPEC's KineticForces module computes the kinetic energy δWK and the neoclassical toroidal viscosity (NTV) torque Tφ from the action variation δJ of each particle class, in the action-angle form of Park (2011) as implemented in PENT (Logan 2013). The bounce average that produces δJ is taken along a flux surface: a particle's orbit is assumed to stay on the surface ψ that labels it. This is the zero-orbit-width (ZOW) approximation. For thermal ions in present devices the trapped-orbit width Δb is a few percent of the minor radius a; for a 3.5 MeV alpha in a reactor-scale tokamak it is 0.1–0.2 a (table below), comparable with the scale of the 3D field it averages.

The project adds a second evaluation of δJ, along the guiding-center orbit in the constants of motion (E, μ, Pφ), feeding the existing resonance operator, energy integral and torque identity, and reducing to the present path as Δb → 0. The paper is a Letter with two results: the alpha torque in ARC, ITER and SPARC versus orbit width (KR1), and the thermal-ion torque density in DIII-D with and without the rational-surface and edge peaks of the ZOW calculation (KR2). A methods figure (KR3) shows the ZOW limit.

What changed

No code changed in this milestone. The diagram shows where the new path enters. The orbit node (dashed, shaded) is new in M1–M2; it reads the same equilibrium and the same δB/B and ∇·ξ⊥ modes as BounceAveraging, at the orbit's (ψ(t), θ(t)) instead of on one surface, and supplies the bounce frequency, the secular toroidal rate ⟨ζ̇⟩ and |δJ|² to the existing resonance operator. The drive changes from ∂f/∂ψ to ∂f/∂Pφ in M3.

Reading

The figure shows the two features KR2 is about. Spikes at rational surfaces are present when the hand regularization is small, and the torque is concentrated at the edge, where the largest structures sit within a banana width of a rational or a kinetic resonance surface, or both. Each spike is one to two orders of magnitude narrower than the local thermal-ion banana width. An orbit average over that width would therefore change the profile's shape, which is the premise of KR2.

The figure also shows something the tracker does not state. At the regularization the example ships with (σ = 0.05), the core rational spikes are already gone, replaced by steps and notches, so "the ZOW profile has spikes at rational surfaces" is true of the method, not of the default run. The integrated ZOW torque depends on σ: reducing σ tenfold raises it 3.0× for n = 1 and 2.2× for n = 3, almost all of it from ψN > 0.9. KR2's acceptance criterion compares the orbit-averaged integrated torque with the ZOW one to within 30%. With the ZOW number moving by a factor of 2–3 over one decade of σ, that comparison needs a stated σ for the ZOW reference. The σ-scan panel of KR2 already tests σ-independence of the orbit-averaged result; the 30% criterion is the part that is underspecified. It is recorded in the journal for a card before M5. It is not a card now, because it does not affect M1–M4.

What the figure does not settle: (i) whether the edge structure is physical or an artifact of ψN → 0.995 truncation and the ideal response near the separatrix; the orbit path's lost-orbit fraction (M3) is the bound PLAN.md names for it. (ii) Whether the narrowest widths are resolved: the ψ quadrature is adaptive (300–1320 evaluations), and the widths below 10−3 are at its node spacing. Either way they are far below the banana width. (iii) Any comparison with DIII-D 145117 (Kim 2014: measured ≈ 3 N m). This equilibrium, its profiles and the 1 kA coil current are not that discharge, so the magnitudes here are not a test of anything. The fallback for KR2 remains this example unless Nik supplies the kinetic EFIT (QUESTIONS.md Q1).

The orbit-width table in the Background shows that the reactor alpha widths, 0.13–0.23 a, are well beyond the "few percent" at which KR1 expects the departure to begin. Reaching the small-width end of the KR1 scan by raising B0 and Ip would need a factor of about 10 in field, so in practice the small-width end will come from the artificial energy scan that PLAN.md already lists as KR1's cross-check. PLAN.md M4 now says so (ladder change; the key results are unchanged).

Q4 - Sign off physics.md, with the proposed resolution of each [VERIFY] item

What was found. physics.md v0 has 18 items marked [VERIFY]. The agent checked each against the code and the reference notes (refs/notes/, made from full text). Sixteen have a proposed resolution that does not change the physics. Two are inconsistencies in the text as written:

  1. §3, the δJ integrand A(t). physics.md writes A(t) = v∥∇·ξ⊥ + (δB/B)(2E − 3μB)/(2mv∥), integrated in time. The code's integrand (jvtheta, BounceAveraging.jl:1054) is JB dθ [∇·ξ⊥(v∥/v) + (δB/B)(1 − 3μB/2E)/(v∥/v)], and with dt ∝ JB dθ/v∥ this is ∮dt [(2E − 2μB)∇·ξ⊥ + (2E − 3μB)δB/B]/(2E/v) · const, which is also the bracket of Park 2011 Eq. 13. The physics.md form differs from this by a factor that varies along the orbit (m v∥ on the ∇·ξ⊥ term, 2mv∥ on the δB/B term), so it would not reproduce the ZOW limit. Proposed: A(t) = (2E − 2μB)∇·ξ⊥ + (2E − 3μB)δB/B, with the constant fixed by the ZOW limit (M2 check 1).
  2. §4, ωE in the drive. physics.md sets f0 = fM(E; n(ψ̄), T(ψ̄)) with E the kinetic energy and Φ left out of the orbit, then states that n∂f0/∂Pφ reproduces the numerator (ωE + ω*n + ω*T(x − 3/2)). Without Φ in f0 the ωE term does not appear: in the code it comes from the derivative at fixed total energy (Logan 2013 Eq. 18 states fixed total energy). Proposed: f0 a function of E + ZeΦ(ψ̄), so ∂f0/∂ψ̄ carries −ZeΦ′/T and the ωE term; the orbit itself is still computed without Φ (as physics.md proposes).

Q5 - Which ARC equilibrium is "ARC" in key result 1?

What was found. The OpenFUSIONToolkit (OFT) TokaMaker "ARC" example is ARC V3A from Hillesheim et al., J. Plasma Phys. (2026): B0 = 11.4 T, Ip = 12.0 MA, R0 = 4.62 m, a = 1.18 m (§1 of that paper). Its notebook downloads the gEQDSK and an input.gacode profile file from Zenodo record 19498373, which is reachable from feynman. The tracker's orbit-width figure used the older Sorbom 2015 ARC (R0 3.3 m, 9.2 T). The alpha orbit widths differ little (0.13 a vs 0.15 a, Background table), so the choice is about which machine the paper names.

Why this is yours to decide. It fixes the device named in the abstract, and only Nik knows whether a CFS-provided equilibrium is available or preferred.

Q6 - Regression target for the zero-orbit-width limit, and the ignored torque keys

What was found. M2's first check compares the new path against today's path at vanishing orbit width. Two choices fix that comparison. (i) The case: the DIII-D-like example (deuterium, n = 1 C-coil, the case in the figure above; 67 s for the full run) or the analytic TJ example (TJ_kinetic_NTV_example, circular, fast). (ii) The post-PerturbedEquilibrium torque path ignores three [KineticForces] keys: nutype, f0type and collisionality_factor are never passed to calculate_gar (Compute.jl:142–147, Torque.jl:177–186), so every torque today runs with the harmonic collision model and a Maxwellian whatever the deck says. The new path needs f0type to select the slowing-down alphas (M3), and a ZOW comparison is only meaningful if both paths see the same keys.

Why this is yours to decide. Fixing (ii) changes results for any user deck that sets a non-default value of those keys, which is a change to develop outside this project's scope.
