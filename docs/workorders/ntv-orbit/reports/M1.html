Question

Can GPEC compute the real guiding-center orbit of an ion in constants of motion (energy E, magnetic moment μ, canonical toroidal momentum Pφ), classify it, and give its bounce frequency, toroidal rate and orbit-averaged flux, such that at vanishing orbit width it reproduces what today's surface-local (zero-orbit-width, ZOW) bounce average uses? And does it keep the two pieces of physics Nik asked for in the Q4 sign-off: finite β (∇p) and the electrostatic potential Φ?

What changed

A new file src/KineticForces/Orbits.jl (diagnostic only; no torque changes). It integrates Littlejohn's guiding-center equations with H = ½mv∥² + μB + ZeΦ(ψ) in time, in (ψN, θ, φ) with θ the equilibrium's straight-field-line angle and φ the geometric toroidal angle. ∇×b comes from the covariant bi of the equilibrium metric, so curvature, its ∇p part and the parallel current enter as the equilibrium has them; there is no low-β drift formula. Φ is built from the kinetic file's ωE. With orbit_diagnostic = true, the run writes KineticForces/Orbits (class, ωb, toroidal rate, ⟨ψ⟩ on a (ψ, λ, x, σ) sample). physics.md is now v1 (signed with Nik's two exceptions resolved, the A(t) correction, and the beam distribution of Q7).

Reading

The ZOW limit holds for the frequencies, and the old precession is not low-β. For trapped ions the orbit's toroidal rate matches today's ωD to 3×10−4 at x = 10−4 (3×10−6 after averaging σ). The orbit uses the full ∇×b of the equilibrium (including ∇p), and today's precession integrand (g_wd) is the ψ-derivative of the bounce action, which does not assume low β. A low-β drift would differ by O(βp), a few percent here. The M0 card said the old path used the low-β form; that was wrong, and physics.md v1 says so. Nik's ∇p exception is therefore met by the orbit as written. The δJ bracket keeps ∇p too: the code builds δB/B from the Lagrangian identity with p′ inside κ.

Sign conventions, now fixed numerically. Inside GPEC's equilibrium representation F > 0 and the poloidal field sign s = −1, so Bt is antiparallel to Ip and "co-current" is v∥F s > 0, i.e. v∥ < 0 here. Today's ωD and ωE both map to the geometric toroidal rate with the factor s. The new path will use that map in M2.

The passing-ion precession is not separable at its own order. For passing ions ωb converges as for trapped ones, but ⟨φ̇⟩ − q ωb does not converge to today's ωDx: it differs by a factor of order one. The difference scales as x, like ωD itself, and is the same for both signs of v∥. It depends on which flux label carries the q: the time average ⟨ψ⟩, the θ-average, or the field line followed along the orbit each give a different answer. The parallel advance q ωb is about 103 times the precession, so a first-order shift of the q-label (Δψ ~ ρ) is the same size as the precession. Physically, the passing resonance ℓ + nq ≈ 0 sits off the rational surface by about an orbit width. This is the effect key result 2 is about, not a defect. Liu 2014 drops a magnetic-shear term for passing particles at this order. Consequence: M2's ZOW-limit check should test passing particles on the ψ-integrated torque (where a label shift integrates out), not surface by surface. The trapped part can be tested surface by surface.

The electric field in this example is strong. At the example's full ωE (4–6×104 rad/s in the core), the E×B speed is 15–35% of the thermal speed at x = 0.3–1. A λ = 0.97 ion at ψN = 0.3 that is deeply trapped without Φ becomes passing with it. The potential changes the local kinetic energy by a sizable fraction of T across one orbit. This is outside the ordering the old path assumes (E×B speed ≪ thermal), and it is where Nik's second exception matters. Including Φ changes the thermal orbit topology, not just its frequency. Where the orbit stays trapped, the rate shift is s·ωE to 1.4%. This bears on KR2: the edge part of the profile will depend on whether Φ is in the orbit, which is the subject of card Q8.

Cost. About 3 ms per thermal orbit (Vern9, rtol 10−10, a few hundred steps); 270 orbits took 8 s including compilation. The M2 grid (about 100 ψ × 64 λ × 20 x × 2 σ, 2.6×105 orbits) is then of order 10 minutes single-threaded before δJ, so threading over ψ will be needed. That is within the M0 budget estimate, so no grid-cost card is expected.

Decisions

Q8 - The potential in the orbit: default for the paper's thermal-ion results

What was found. Your Q4 exceptions are met as follows. ∇p: the orbit uses the full equilibrium curvature, and the old precession is already β-exact (Reading). Φ: the orbit carries ZeΦ(ψ) in its Hamiltonian, built from the kinetic file's ωE. The constant-of-motion form makes this almost free, and f₀(E − ZeΦ(ψ̄)) then supplies the ωE drive term (physics.md v1 §4). In the DIII-D-like example, ωE is large enough (vE/v up to 0.35) that Φ changes which thermal orbits are trapped. KR2's orbit-averaged edge profile will therefore differ between Φ on and Φ off, and the paper has to say which is the result.

Also on this card. physics.md v1 §2 states the ZOW limit as ⟨φ̇⟩ → ±qωb + ωDx + ωE. M1 shows the correct trapped form is s·(ωDx + ωE), and that the passing form is label-dependent at first order (Reading). Any answer to Q8 also approves this wording correction to physics.md, with no change to the method. A note in the textbox can withhold it.

Why this is yours to decide. It sets what key result 2 claims for the edge. Φ on is the more complete model, but it also moves the result further from the ZOW reference, which has Φ only as a frequency.

KR2 states the orbit-averaged result with the electrostatic orbit modification and shows how much of the change comes from Φ. One extra run per KR2 case.
Cleaner separation of the orbit-width effect from the Er effect, but the headline edge result omits physics the example shows to be first order.
Simplest figure, but a reviewer cannot tell orbit width from Er.
Recommendation: A, fairly sure. The switch exists (orbit_potential), the cost is one run, and showing both answers the reviewer question in advance. Not gating: M2 runs Φ off for its ZOW-limit check either way. The answer is needed by M5.
