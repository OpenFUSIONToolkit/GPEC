M3 turns forb, the NTV torque computed along each particle's guiding-center orbit, into a method that can handle fusion alphas. It weights each orbit by its phase-space measure (your Q14 A). It drives each orbit by the gradient of the distribution at the orbit's own mean flux. It adds the slowing-down distribution of fusion alphas to both methods, and it excludes lost orbits and counts them. This report also gives the first alpha torque on an ARC-class equilibrium.

In brief. The measure and drive keep the zero-orbit-width (ZOW) limit clean: the M2 scan still falls as a power law, slope 0.96, with no floor, once a floor that I first missed was fixed (Section 2). The thermal-ion torque keeps its sign; it grows from 0.119 to 0.147 N m with the potential on. The alpha distribution reproduces its analytic density and pressure moments, and its fusion source matches the ARC transport file's to 0.5%. On ARC, with q = 1 kept out of the response, the alpha torque is about 1.5 N m on both methods. The difference is in its shape: the orbit average removes the surface method's peak between the q = 3 and q = 4 surfaces. Two problems surfaced. On the way I found a develop bug that integrated the torque over a region with no plasma response; it is fixed in a draft PR. With q = 1 inside the response, the orbit method shows a negative energy band about one alpha orbit wide around q = 1, which I have not resolved. Three cards: the lost-fraction check (Q16), when and how to add beams (Q17), and how M4 should treat q < 1 (Q18, gating).

1. What M3 changed, and how it was checked

The object. For each flux surface ψ₀ (ψ the normalized poloidal flux), forb launches guiding-center orbits from the point of minimum field strength θbmin. Each orbit is labelled by its pitch λ = μB₀/E (μ the magnetic moment, E the energy, B₀ a reference field), its normalized speed u = √(E/T) (T the temperature) and the sign σ of its parallel velocity. Its bounce frequency, mean toroidal rate and the bounce harmonics of the perturbed action |δJℓ|² go into the same resonant pitch and energy integrals as the surface method fgar, the existing GPEC calculation on each flux surface. Up to M2, each orbit carried the surface weight and the drive of its start surface ψ₀. M3 changes what each orbit contributes (Figure {{N_WEIGHT}}).

The changes (gpec e1057aca4, e28165e76; physics.md v2 §3–§6):

  1. Measure and drive. Gorelenkov et al. (1999) Eq. 22 writes the phase-space volume as (2π)² Σσ (B/ωc) dPφ dμ dE dt, with ωc the cyclotron frequency (read in the PDF). Labelling orbits by their launch flux ψ₀ therefore brings in the factor |∂Pφ/∂ψ₀| at fixed E and μ. The drive is ∂f₀/∂Pφ at fixed E and μ, with f₀ a function of E and ψ̄. In the static torque the two multiply, and |∂Pφ/∂ψ₀| cancels except for its sign. What remains is (∂f₀/∂ψ̄)·g, with g = ∂ψ̄/∂ψ₀. The code gets g by a central difference from two status-only orbits at ψ₀ ± h, h = 10⁻³, with the same E, μ, σ and θ₀. A neighbour that changes class, or leaves the domain, is retried at h/8 and h/64. If one side still fails, the difference is one-sided; if both do, the sample is dropped. The sign comes from the analytic ∂Pφ/∂ψ₀ at the launch point. Each launch is weighted by 1/(number of times its orbit crosses the launch section), ½ for trapped and 1 for passing orbits, from the crossings each orbit records.
  2. Slowing-down distribution (physics.md §6, isotropic). f₀ ∝ 1/(E3/2 + Ec3/2) below the birth energy Eb, where Ec is the critical energy at which electron and ion drag are equal. It is a new f0type = "slowingdown" on both methods, with analytic energy and ψ derivatives. A new deck key alpha = true adds an alpha species (Z = 2, A = 4) to the species sum. Its density follows from the D–T fusion source and the slowing-down time (Section 3).
  3. Lost orbits. Orbits that reach the last surface of the response (ψlim) contribute nothing. The lost share of each surface's launch phase space goes to HDF5 as KineticForces/forb/lost_fraction.
  4. forb is no longer cut near the axis. The surface method is suppressed inside an orbit-width validity boundary (physics.md §1); the orbit method has no such limit and now integrates from the first surface.

How it was checked. Before writing the code, an independent review agent re-derived the measure, the drive and the slowing-down bracket from physics.md and the papers, without my derivation (results/m3_dev/review_m3_algebra.md). It confirmed all three. The one error it found, the sign of one slowing-down term, had already been caught and corrected. Unit tests check ∂Pφ/∂ψ₀ against a finite difference (relative 10⁻⁶ at Z = 1 and 4096), check that the drive at ψ̄ = ψ₀ equals the surface numerator, and check the distribution's moments (Section 3). A new deck-and-API parity case runs the alpha species on both methods.

2. The measure and the drive

Zero orbit width. Raising the charge Z shrinks the orbit width Δb ∝ 1/Z, so forb must approach the replay: the surface values of fgar pushed through the same orbit integrals on the same pitch grid. Variant A raises Z only; variant B also divides the E×B frequency ωE and the collision frequency ν by Z. With the measure and the drive in, both fall with no floor (Figure {{N_ZOW}}): A as Δb0.96, and B down to 6.6×10⁻⁷, the M2 value. The new drive brings ψ̄ and g into every orbit; both go to their surface values as 1/Z.

A floor I first missed. My journal called the first M3 scan floor-free, with variant B down to 6.7×10⁻⁷. The finished files say otherwise: B goes flat at 2×10⁻⁵ from Z = 4096 on. I cannot reproduce the journal's numbers from them. Most likely I read the files before every task had finished; that is inference. The rows each task writes last, the surfaces nearest ψlim, carry the whole floor: without the last two, B falls to 6.7×10⁻⁷. At those surfaces the outward neighbour at ψ₀ + h leaves the response, and 24 samples per surface were dropped at every Z, so the inward one failed too. My reading, not checked sample by sample, is that at fixed λ the inward neighbour sees a different field at θ₀, and the pitch rows next to the trapped–passing boundary change class there. Retrying a failed side at h/8 and h/64 (gpec 417d4df65) removes the floor: no sample is dropped from Z = 4096 on. Elsewhere it changes the integrated torque by at most 2×10⁻³ (at Z = 16) and by under 10⁻⁵ from Z = 64 on.

One-sided differences. The tables now count the samples they keep. In the scan, 2–7% of the g samples are one-sided for Z ≤ 1024 and under 0.4% above it; at most 0.1% are dropped. The one-sided ones sit where finite width moves the class boundary in energy. The scan is the test that they do no harm.

The thermal-ion torque, potential on. At Z = 1 with the electrostatic potential Φ in the orbit (your Q8 default), the orbit torque grows from 0.119 + 0.077i N m at M2 to 0.147 + 0.136i N m, with the same sign (Figure {{N_PROFILE}}). The edge peaks of fgar stay removed. The step fix moved this total by under 1%. With Φ on, 8.4% of the 795,138 kept samples are one-sided and 0.4% are dropped. My inference, untested: with Φ on, a neighbour launched at the same total energy has a different kinetic energy, so more orbits near the E×B-modified class boundaries change class. This matters for KR2, not for the alpha runs of M4, which have no potential; M5 opens with a test at h = 10⁻⁴.

3. Slowing-down alphas

The distribution. With x = E/Ec and xb = Eb/Ec, the code uses f̂ = (3√π/4L)/(x3/2 + 1) for x < xb, with L = ln(1 + xb3/2). Its density moment is 1 by construction, and its pressure moment is p/(n Ec) = (xb − I)/L, with I = ∫₀xb dx/(1 + x3/2) in closed form. Quadrature of the code's weight reproduces both to 10⁻¹⁰ (unit tests). The species density is the steady state of the fusion source, nα = S τs L/3, with S = nDnT⟨σv⟩ from the Bosch–Hale fit and τs the Spitzer slowing-down time on electrons. Ec = 14.8 A Te(Σj njZj²/(neAj))2/3 sums over the resolved ions, including the impurity that closes quasi-neutrality. The collision model is the harmonic one, with the alpha's pitch-angle scattering rate on the ions at the critical speed as its rate; its x−3/2 is the fast-ion v−3 law.

Against the ARC transport file. The ARC V3A case (your Q5 A; gEQDSK and MITIM input.gacode, Zenodo 19498373) carries its own fast-helium density and fusion power. The source S agrees with the file's qfus/Eα to 0.5% at every radius. On axis, nα and the energy per particle (1.0 MeV) agree within 3% (Figure {{N_MOMENTS}}). Off axis, the file's fast helium is 30–70% denser. In the model, nα/S falls from 0.27 s on axis to 0.08 s at ψ = 0.96, following τs ∝ Te3/2/ne. In the file it stays near 0.25 s. That points to radial transport or a global slowing-down time in the file's model; I have not found its documentation. This is a comparison with another model, not the PLAN check, which is the moment test above.

4. Lost orbits

The lost fraction is the density-weighted share of a surface's launch phase space whose orbits reach ψlim. On ARC it is zero inside ψ = 0.8, rises monotonically and ends at 0.4999 on the last surface (Figure {{N_LOST}}). PLAN asks for 1. That cannot happen for a launch-section count. An orbit started on the last surface stays inside only if its launch point is its outermost point. At θbmin that holds for one of the two signs of v∥. For a passing orbit the drift shifts one direction outward and the other inward. A trapped orbit is launched on one leg of its banana, and the other leg lies outward for one σ and inward for the other. So the limit is ½, and the run reaches it to 10⁻⁴. PLAN's "1" holds for orbits whose mean flux ψ̄ is at the edge. That is physics.md §5's wording, L(ψ̄), but a lost orbit never closes and has no ψ̄.

5. The ARC alpha torque

The case. ARC V3A (B₀ = 11.4 T, Ip = 12 MA, a = 1.18 m) with D and T at 50/50 of the fuel density, boron closing quasi-neutrality and the alpha species, all on the same n = 1 perturbation. The perturbation is the plasma response to an applied normal field of 10⁻³ T on m = 2, 3, 4. ARC's own error field is M4's business; this is a test case. The file has no rotation, so ωE = 0 and Φ plays no part.

A bug on the way. ARC has q₀ = 0.89. With the default qlow = 1.02, ForceFreeStates (FFS), the stability stage, starts its response at q = 1.02, ψ = 0.343. KineticForces still integrated the torque from ψ = 10⁻⁴, so everything inside 0.343 came from extrapolated perturbation data. The first alpha torque, 6306 N m, was nearly all extrapolation. The torque quadrature now starts where the response starts, as it already stopped where it ends. The fix went to develop as draft PR #661; the harness shows the five kinetic cases unchanged. The M2 DIII-D runs were not affected, since their q₀ = 1.20.

q = 1 kept out. With the fix and qlow = 1.02, the response and the torque cover ψ = 0.343–0.994 (Figure {{N_ALPHA}}). The two methods give nearly the same alpha torque: fgar 1.35 + 7.73i N m and forb 1.49 + 7.10i N m. The fuel ions give 8.9 and 8.6 N m on fgar. The difference is in the shape. The fgar peak at ψ = 0.96, between the q = 3 and q = 4 surfaces, is gone from forb; this is KR2's mechanism showing up for alphas. Near the inner edge of the response, forb rises to twice fgar. An alpha orbit's width in ψ there is a few hundredths, so orbits launched from the first surfaces reach below 0.343, onto perturbation data that is extrapolated again. The first ~0.03 in ψ is not trustworthy.

Resolution. I reran half of the surfaces at 128 pitch × 16 energy nodes. The torque on them changes by 2%. The energy term rises by 7%, by about the same amount on every surface (5–20%, most at the edge). The 8% gap between the methods' energy terms is the same size, so it is not a result yet. The torque difference in shape is well above both changes. M4's scans will use 128 × 16 and check one point at 192 × 24.

q = 1 inside. With qlow = 0.8 the response covers the core, q = 1 at ψ = 0.313 included. All free-boundary n = 1 modes are stable, but the fixed-boundary test flags the n = 1 mode as unstable: the internal kink of a q₀ < 1 plasma. The largest island half-width grows from 0.017 to 0.106. On fgar the alpha torque is 5.24 + 20.6i N m, most of it inside q = 1. On forb the real part agrees at 6.8 N m. The energy term, however, turns strongly negative in a band about ±0.03 around q = 1 (Figure {{N_Q1}}). Doubling the pitch and energy resolution leaves the band in place: −110 against −102 at ψ = 0.289. So the band comes from the response's ψ structure, not from the energy or pitch grids. Elsewhere the coarse 32×8 grid was not converged: the total energy term changes sign between 32×8 and 64×12.

I probed the response across q = 1 on a fine grid. The δB/B harmonics jump at q = 1; m = 2, for one, falls 30×. Within 0.0015 of the surface they overshoot their median 4–10×, which is a cubic spline ringing at a jump. The overshoot is much narrower than an alpha orbit. The jump is the ideal response's own: an orbit that straddles q = 1 samples both sides, while fgar sees one side at a time. I cannot yet say whether the band is that physics, the ringing, or the near-marginal internal kink that sets the size of the jump. For KR1 this is a question of which equilibria to use.

ITER and SPARC baseline scenarios also have q₀ near or below 1, so M4's TokaMaker families will meet the same choice.

6. Beams

Your Q7 B asked that the slowing-down species also take the MARS-K beam form of Liu et al. (2014) Eqs. 6–7, a Gaussian in the pitch label ζ about the injection pitch ζ₀, widening as the ions slow down. physics.md §6 defines ζ = σ√(1 − λ), which is v∥/v where B = B₀. That label is not defined for λ > 1. Those are orbits trapped on the outboard side, where B < B₀, and every ARC and DIII-D surface outside the axis has them. A pitch label must be a constant of motion and defined for every confined orbit. ζ² = 1 − λBmin(ψ̄)/B₀ is both: v∥/v at the orbit's own field minimum. It reduces to Liu's label on the axis. I have not written the beam form: it needs this definition first, and the alpha physics had to settle before beams could reuse it.

7. Cost (your Q15 hybrid)

You chose to do the remaining speed-ups in M3 only if the alpha cost asked for them, and otherwise to revisit them in M7. It does not ask. An ARC alpha profile of 39 surfaces at 64 pitch × 12 energy nodes takes 0.91 h of table and 0.54 h of integrals on one CPU; each surface takes 1.5–1.9 min of table. At 128 × 16 nodes, which Section 5 asks for, a profile costs 1.7 times more, about 2.5 h. M4 then needs about 3 machines × 6 field values × 2.5 h ≈ 45 CPU-hours, about 3 hours on 16 CPUs. The thermal-ion table with Φ on has tripled to 3.5 h, because of the neighbour orbits for g, but it is needed once per KR2 profile. The speed-ups stay parked for the M7 performance review.
