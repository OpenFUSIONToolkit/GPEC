Field strength and displacement gauge

Verified at Julia base 0304fd54c. xi_s is the surface displacement of Glasser (2016), PoP 23 072505, Eq. 9:

\[\xi_s=\chi_1(q\xi^\theta-\xi^\zeta),\qquad \chi_1=2\pi\psi_o,\qquad \xi^\alpha=\xi_s/\chi_1.\]

Eq. 18 describes elimination of this displacement in the Euler-Lagrange system. Calling it a toroidal displacement confuses it with xi^zeta. The corrected docstrings preserve the definition and numerical behavior. See src/PerturbedEquilibrium/FieldReconstruction.jl and the conversion to clebsch_alpha before the geometric matrix products in src/KineticForces/KineticForcesStructs.jl:375.

Verified. The kinetic matrix input uses the perpendicular displacement: dbob=delta B_L(xi_perp)/B=-(divx+kapx) (src/KineticForces/KineticForcesStructs.jl:437). The curvature matrices supply kapx=kappa.xi_perp, and the divergence matrices supply divx after removing their J B^2 weight. FortSym G7 and G8 verify these normalizations, including the xi_s/chi1 conversion. The paired dbob,divx is the correct contract for the explicit Jacobian term in the general kinetic action; it is not by itself the input to a code that accepts only perturbed-Boozer field strength. Park (2011), PoP 18 110702, Eqs. 4, 10 and 11, and Logan and Park (2013), PoP 20 122507, Eqs. 4 and 5, give those contracts.

The CGL path separately used kapx=-(dbob+divx)/2 at src/KineticForces/Torque.jl:295. Verified defect: the extra half is inconsistent with the matrix input. Its correction and independent pressure-work regression are a separate topic branch.

For comparison, Fortran GPEC's pmodb lagb is

\[b_g=\delta B_E+\xi^\psi\partial_\psi B+ \frac{\xi_s}{\chi_1 q}\partial_\theta B, \qquad b_g-b_\perp=-\frac{\xi_\perp^\zeta}{q}\partial_\theta B.\]

It uses xi_g^zeta=0 in the working chart; jac_out only relabels harmonics. Fortran diagnostic llagb is b_perp=delta B_E+xi_perp.grad B. Under ideal induction and equilibrium force balance, b_perp=-B(div xi_perp+kappa.xi_perp). With a force residual F=(curl B) cross B-mu0 grad p, the force-balance curvature representation instead leaves -xi_perp.F/B. Regularized numerical outputs need not be an exact ideal-induction pair near a rational surface. In the diagonal Jacobian model the reconstructed surface displacement is R xi_s, and the legacy llagb differs from the reconstructed perpendicular projection by (1-R) xi_s/(chi1 q) partial_theta B (native proofs R4-R5). The llagb identification above assumes unregularized components.

Verified target-gauge equations. In angles in turns put D=partial_theta+q partial_zeta. On a matching equilibrium chart J B^a=H(psi), a=0 for Hamada and a=2 for Boozer. Given one consistent pair b_X,d_X, form T=d_X+a b_X/B and solve

\[D w=-T,\qquad w_m=\frac{iT_m}{2\pi(m-nq)},\qquad b_{target}=b_X+w\partial_\theta B.\]

A nonzero resonant source has no periodic solution. For a Hamada scalar already relabeled onto Boozer equilibrium angles, T=2 b_H/B; this is the rmptorque c3614334 Eq. 4.14 kernel. Relabeling the equilibrium chart and changing the perturbed displacement gauge are distinct operations. For PEST, J/R^2 is a flux function and its linearized constraint is `div xi-2 xiR/R`; there is no automatic Boozer/Hamada interpretation.

Verification: run PYTHONPATH=$FORTSYM/python python3 gen/pmodb_gauge/derive.py. All accepted identities use FortSym's native zero test (ae635c80), with no numerical fallback. fortsym_verdicts.json records the exact labels. The optional direct curvilinear contraction G6 remains UNKNOWN for the large rational metric jet; C1/C2 prove the same invariant relation on Cartesian jets and G3-G5 map it to the columns. Negative controls have exact rational nonzero witnesses. #84/#85/#86 are available; accepted proofs do not depend on the open coefficient/radical gaps #91/#92.

Hypothesis limit: the original ITER edge discrepancies cannot be quantitatively assigned to force error, interpolation or regularization without an independent equilibrium-residual calculation. The pressure coefficient near 0.168 is explained numerically by a missing division by B in the old fitting regressor, plus the omitted parallel gauge term; the correct pressure coefficient is one (FortSym P1).

The general-J continuum budget R6-R7 additionally separates the ordinary gauge term, the reconstructed surface-displacement defect, the difference between the derivatives used in the field and the output, and the force residual. When both paths use the same derivative its explicit contribution to the column mismatch cancels, even for a regularized derivative. A large Bdivxprp by itself therefore does not determine the net error budget.
