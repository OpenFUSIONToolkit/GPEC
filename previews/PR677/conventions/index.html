Conventions Reference

The sign, coordinate and field-amplitude conventions used throughout GPEC, for reading its output and interfacing it with other codes. They are inherited from the Fortran GPEC suite and predate the COCOS standard (see COCOS compatibility); each section names the source that establishes the convention.

Coordinate system

GPEC uses right-handed magnetic coordinates $(\psi, \theta, \zeta)$ with the Fourier kernel

\[\exp\!\big(i(m\theta - n\zeta)\big),\]

matching the Fortran $\exp(im\theta - in\phi)$ convention, with the angles in radians in the kernel ($2\pi$ times the unit-period $\theta$ and $\zeta$ below). The analysis transform that produces the mode coefficients $f_m$ of a profile $f(\theta)$ uses the conjugate kernel $\exp(-im\theta)$, and the synthesis reconstructs with $\exp(+im\theta)$ (Utilities.FourierTransforms).

Poloidal flux $\psi$

  • Normalized from 0 (magnetic axis) to 1 (plasma boundary); $\psi_0 = |\psi_\mathrm{bry} - \psi_\mathrm{axis}|$ is forced positive on read, and an EQDSK with $\psi_\mathrm{bry} < \psi_\mathrm{axis}$ has its flux array sign-flipped so that $\psi$ increases outward (Equilibrium.Sources.GFile).
  • The internal $\psi$ carries no factor of $2\pi$ (poloidal flux per radian); IMAS inputs, which use the full flux, are divided by $2\pi$ on read.
  • $\rho = \sqrt{\psi}$ serves as a radius-like variable where one is needed.

Poloidal angle $\theta$

  • Upward outboard, always: $\theta = 0$ on the outboard midplane, increasing upward.
  • Normalized to increase by 1, not $2\pi$, over one poloidal circuit, whatever the helicity or working coordinate.

Toroidal coordinate $\zeta$ and $\phi$

  • The magnetic toroidal angle is $\zeta = (\phi - \nu(\psi,\theta))/(2\pi)$, with $\phi$ the internal toroidal angle and $\nu$ the single-valued straight-field-line offset of the working coordinate ($\nu = 0$ in PEST coordinates).
  • The physical (laboratory) toroidal angle is $\phi_\mathrm{lab} = -\,\mathrm{helicity}\,(2\pi\zeta + \nu)$ (ForcingTerms), a different angle from the internal $\phi$ above, so $\phi_\mathrm{lab}$ runs counter-clockwise viewed from above for left-handed configurations and clockwise for right-handed ones.

[Equilibrium] jac_type selects the Jacobian $J \propto B_p^{p_{bp}}\, B^{p_b}\, R^{-p_r}\, r_c^{-p_{rc}}$, with $B_p$ the poloidal field, $B$ the total field, $R$ the major radius and $r_c$ the distance from the magnetic axis:

Helicity and handedness

Helicity is the product of the toroidal-field and plasma-current directions, helicity = bt_sign * sign(crnt) (ForcingTerms): +1 is right-handed ($B_t$ and $I_p$ parallel), $-1$ left-handed (opposed). It sets the direction of the laboratory toroidal grid, $\phi_{\mathrm{lab},j} = -\mathrm{helicity}\times 2\pi j/n_\zeta$, and the output sign flips of Spectrum output sign conventions; for DIII-D ($B_t < 0$, $I_p > 0$, helicity $-1$) $\phi_\mathrm{lab}$ increases with $j$.

$F = R B_\phi$

The poloidal-current function is forced positive on read, $F = |F|$ (Equilibrium.Sources.GFile); the sign of $B_t$ is carried separately by fpol_sign and the helicity inputs.

Safety factor $q$

The direct solver computes $q$ by field-line integration and overwrites the file profile (Equilibrium.Axisymmetric.Direct),

\[q = \frac{F}{2\pi}\oint \frac{J}{B_p}\,dl_p ,\]

which is positive because $J > 0$ and $F = |F|$; a warning marks an on-axis extrapolation with $q_0 \le 0$ as a spline artifact. Inverse equilibria use the $q$ profile supplied with the input (Equilibrium.Axisymmetric.Inverse), the convention of the Fortran GPEC CHEASE reader.

Mode numbers $m$ and $n$

Toroidal mode number $n$

[ForceFreeStates] nn is a positive integer or a list of consecutive integers. $n < 1$ is not supported: a run whose modes are all below 1 is an error, and a lowest mode below 1 is clamped to 1 with a warning (ForceFreeStates.Run). Results for several $n$ can be superposed.

Poloidal mode range

The poloidal spectrum spans mlow to mhigh (ForceFreeStates.ForceBalance):

\[m_\mathrm{low} = \min(\lfloor n_\mathrm{low}\,q_\mathrm{min}\rfloor,\,0) - 4 - \Delta m_\mathrm{low}, \qquad m_\mathrm{high} = \lfloor n_\mathrm{high}\,q_\mathrm{max}\rfloor + 2\,\Delta m_\mathrm{high}.\]

delta_mlow and delta_mhigh widen the range beyond the resonant modes; delta_mhigh is doubled before use, the Fortran DCON convention, so it adds twice as many modes on the high side as delta_mlow does on the low side.

Why positive $m$ is always resonant

Resonant surfaces are the flux surfaces where $m = n\,q(\psi)$ (ForceFreeStates.Surfaces). Since $n > 0$ by convention and $q > 0$ for a standard tokamak, the resonant $m$ is always positive, $m_\mathrm{res} = n\,q > 0$, and negative-$m$ modes are never resonant: the $m = 2$ displacement resonates at $q = 2$.

Spectrum output sign conventions

For the real-space representation GPEC takes the complex conjugate of the mode amplitudes for right-handed configurations (Analysis.PerturbedEquilibriumModes), after the straight-field-line offset has entered as $\exp(i\,n\,\nu)$. For the full Fourier representation $\exp(i(m\theta - n\zeta))$ the conjugation flips both the toroidal direction and the up/down sense.

Interfacing with SURFMN

SURFMN expands in $\exp(-im\theta - in\phi)$ with CCW $\phi$; to convert from GPEC,

m_surfmn = helicity * m_gpec
b_surfmn = real(b_m) - 1j * helicity * imag(b_m)

so that for LH configurations only the sign of $m$ is flipped and for RH the complex conjugate is taken.

Interfacing with Vacuum

The Vacuum module works in the same angles as the rest of GPEC, $\theta$ upward-outboard and the analysis basis $\exp(-i(m\theta - n\zeta))$ in radians, unlike the Fortran VACUUM code whose $\theta$ and $\phi$ both run the other way; a clockwise plasma contour is rejected and a clockwise wall file is reversed with a warning. The $\nu$ passed to it is GPEC's $\nu = \phi - 2\pi\zeta$, which Chance (1997, Eq. 97) writes as $\zeta = \phi + \nu$.

Field amplitudes and units

GPEC reports perturbed fields as Fourier spectra on flux surfaces. A raw spectrum depends on the working coordinate (Park, Boozer and Menard (2008)), but the resonant component on a rational surface and the 2-norm of the spectrum on any surface become coordinate-invariant with the right area weighting in the Fourier integrand (Pharr et al. (2026)). GPEC therefore works with three decompositions of the normal field, all in tesla, distinguished only by that weight:

Pharr symbolIntegrand weightNameHDF5 token
$b$ (bare)$1$normal fieldb_n
$\bar b$ (bar)$\mathcal{J}\,\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert$area-weighted fieldarea
$\tilde b$ (tilde)$\sqrt{\mathcal{J}\,\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert}$root-area-weighted fieldroot_area

\[b_m = \oint (\mathbf{b}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n})\, e^{-i m\theta}\, d\theta, \qquad \bar b_m = \frac{1}{A}\oint \mathcal{J}\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert\,(\mathbf{b}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n})\, e^{-i m\theta}\, d\theta, \qquad \tilde b_m = \frac{1}{\sqrt{A}}\oint \sqrt{\mathcal{J}\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert}\,(\mathbf{b}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n})\, e^{-i m\theta}\, d\theta ,\]

with $A$ the scalar surface area. The root-area weighting is the unique one under which the spectrum transforms unitarily between working coordinates, so $\sum_m |\tilde b_m|^2 = \langle (\mathbf{b}\cdot\hat{\mathbf n})^2 \rangle$ (Parseval) and the quantities derived from $\tilde b$, such as the singular values of the resonant coupling matrix, are coordinate-invariant; "power-normalized field" in early GPEC literature names this property. The area weighting is coordinate-invariant for the pitch-resonant $m = nq$ harmonic on a rational surface: the resonant field $\bar b^{\,r} = \Phi^r/A^r$, the resonant flux divided by the surface area, stored as PerturbedEquilibrium/SingularCoupling/resonant_area_weighted_field. In an ideal perturbed equilibrium the surface is perfectly shielded and the stored value is the effective resonant field, the resonant flux the shielding current cancels; resistive and kinetic equilibria have finite resonant fields as well.

With $\Sigma$ the mode-space square-root-weight convolution (sqrtamat) and $A$ the surface area (jarea), the three fields and the flux are related by (Equilibrium.CoordinateInvariant)

\[\tilde b = \Sigma\, b, \qquad \bar b = (\Sigma/\sqrt{A})\,\tilde b, \qquad \Phi = A\,\bar b = \Sigma\sqrt{A}\;\tilde b ,\]

where rootarea_to_area_weight $= \Sigma/\sqrt{A}$ maps $\tilde b \to \bar b$, area_to_rootarea_weight is its inverse, and $R = \Sigma\sqrt{A}$ is the internal flux-conform operator. Flux $\Phi$ is never stored: it appears only when a user supplies flux-valued forcing and is recovered on demand as $\Phi = A\,\bar b$.

Field representations in GPEC output

  • PerturbedEquilibrium/forcing_b, forcing_b_root_area, forcing_b_area and the response_* triplet: the control-surface spectra in the bare, root-area-weighted and area-weighted representations.
  • PerturbedEquilibrium/Response/b_n and b_psi_area_weighted: the bare normal field and the area-weighted radial field profiles.
  • PerturbedEquilibrium/SingularCoupling/resonant_area_weighted_field, C_resonant_area_weighted_field and penetrated_area_weighted_field: the resonant area-weighted field, its coupling matrix and the penetrated field.
  • PerturbedEquilibrium/ResponseMatrices/: the response matrices in root-area-weighted space, with rootarea_to_area_weight_operator $S$ ($L_{\bar b} = S\,\tilde L\,S^\dagger$) and surface_area $A$ ($\Phi = A\,\bar b$) to recover the other forms.
  • ForceFreeStates/FreeBoundaryStability/eigenmode_energies: energies per unit surface-averaged squared displacement, a normalization described on the Stability page.

KineticForces rotation conventions

The rotation profiles enter the KineticForces integrals as follows.

$\omega_E$ (E × B rotation)

$\omega_E$ is the input E × B toroidal rotation profile, positive in the direction of the toroidal coordinate $\zeta$. In the laboratory frame, with $\phi$ counter-clockwise viewed from above, $\phi = -h\,(2\pi\zeta + \nu)$ for helicity $h$ (Helicity and handedness), so positive $\omega_E$ is counter-clockwise with sign $-h$, and its sign relative to the plasma current is $-h\,s_I = -s_B$ with $s_I$, $s_B$ the signs of $I_p$ and $B_t$ (ip_sign, bt_sign): positive $\omega_E$ is co-current exactly when $B_t$ is clockwise ($s_B = -1$), whatever the helicity.

Diamagnetic frequencies

Computed at evaluation time from the kinetic-profile gradients (Logan et al. (2013), Eq. 7):

\[\omega_{*n} = -\frac{2\pi\, T_i}{e\, Z_i\, \chi_1\, n_i}\frac{dn_i}{d\psi_n}, \qquad \omega_{*T} = -\frac{2\pi}{e\, Z_i\, \chi_1}\frac{dT_i}{d\psi_n},\]

with $\chi_1 = 2\pi\psi_0$, so that an outward-decreasing density or temperature gives a positive diamagnetic frequency; the total toroidal rotation is $\omega_\phi = \omega_E + \omega_{*n} + \omega_{*T}$.

Energy integral resonance

The resonance denominator of the energy integral is $i\,\Omega(x) - \nu$ with

\[\Omega(x) = n\,\omega_E + \ell_\mathrm{eff}\,\omega_b\sqrt{x} + n\,\omega_D\,x , \qquad \ell_\mathrm{eff} = \begin{cases} \ell + n q & \text{passing particles},\\ \ell & \text{trapped particles}, \end{cases}\]

where $x = E/T$ is the normalized energy, $\nu$ the energy-dependent effective collisionality, and $\omega_b$, $\omega_D$ the bounce and precession frequencies at thermal energy ($\omega_b \propto v_\mathrm{th}$, $\omega_D \propto v_\mathrm{th}^2$; BounceAveraging.jl). The sign of $\omega_E$ shifts the resonance in velocity space.

COCOS compatibility

The conventions above are GPEC-native and predate the COCOS standard of Sauter and Medvedev (2013): right-handed $(\psi, \theta, \zeta)$, $\psi$ without the $2\pi$ and increasing outward, $q > 0$, $n > 0$ and $F = |F|$. The code labels this internal convention COCOS 2 (Equilibrium.Sources.IMAS) and accepts IMAS input in two forms:

imas_cocos = 11   # default: IMAS standard, ψ divided by 2π on read
imas_cocos = 2    # data already in the internal convention, no conversion

The conversion from COCOS 11 divides $\psi$ by $2\pi$ and relies on the forced sign conventions above (positive $\psi$, $F$ and $q$) for the rest; no other COCOS index is converted.
